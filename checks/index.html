Find a payroll problem

Each example starts with one item the free checker can return. Pick the question closest to the result you are reviewing. The page shows a sample week, its rate table and the actual checker output, followed by a corrected comparison and a second result.

Names, trades and rates are made up. Use your own work records and the wage determination in your contract for a real week. The sample rates are there to explain the math, not to supply a rate for a county or a project.

  • Why does the certified payroll checker leave apprentice pay unchecked?

    Apprentice pay and overtime follow the registered program, so this tool checks the entered ratio and leaves pay for a separate review.

  • No rate for this trade on certified payroll: why was the row skipped?

    Add a rate-table line that matches the worker's classification before the checker can review that row.

  • Davis-Bacon fringe benefits short: how much is missing from the week?

    Count cash above the base, cash fringe and plan credit before finding any fringe gap.

  • Michigan Sunday or holiday payroll: why does the checker show a double-time note?

    With Michigan mode on, Sunday hours and marked holiday hours get a double-time reminder because the CSV has no double-time wage field.

  • Why does Davis-Bacon overtime use the full cash rate when it includes fringe?

    The checker treats rate_paid as wages and uses that full rate unless cash in place of fringe is entered in its own column.

  • One worker in two trades: why does certified payroll overtime need a hand check?

    The checker totals the worker's hours across trades but leaves the overtime split to the payroll records.

  • Davis-Bacon overtime under time and a half: how do you spot it on certified payroll?

    Compare the overtime wage with one and a half times the basic rate, leaving cash paid in place of fringe separate.

  • Certified payroll overtime not checked: what does a blank overtime rate mean?

    A blank overtime wage means the checker reports the required rate in a note and cannot compare it with what was paid.

  • Davis-Bacon wage below base but benefits cover the total: why is there a note?

    The checker gives a note when cash and plan payments cover base plus fringe even though the wage field is below base.

  • Davis-Bacon pay below the base rate: how do you find the weekly shortfall?

    Add the cash wage, cash fringe and plan credit, then compare that total with the base rate plus fringe.

  • Apprentices over the allowed ratio: how do you find the days that need review?

    Count apprentices and journeyworkers in each trade on each day, then apply the entered program ratio and round down.

  • Does cash pay above the Davis-Bacon base count toward fringe?

    Yes, the checker counts the part of the cash wage above base toward the fringe and shows that credit in a note.

How to read the results

A flag identifies pay that looks short or a daily apprentice count above the entered ratio. A file-or-rates fix means a row could not be checked yet. A note explains an input, a credit or a limit. Some notes need a review by hand even when no dollar gap is shown.

Read the summary counts as well as the messages. A skipped row is different from a checked row. A blank overtime rate is different from a rate that was compared and found high enough. The sample pages keep those distinctions visible in both runs.

Some comparison weeks change an actual work condition, such as the future staffing or schedule. They explain how a note can disappear without pretending that a new CSV repairs past work. Keep the original hours, trade and worker status when reviewing a real payroll.

Rule summaries cite the sources recorded in the project's rule notes, read 8 November 2026. They are a dated summary, not legal advice. The checker does not choose your rate determination, verify benefit plans, confirm apprentice registration or sign the weekly form.
