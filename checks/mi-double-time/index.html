Michigan Sunday or holiday payroll: why does the checker show a double-time note?

With Michigan mode on, Sunday hours and marked holiday hours get a double-time reminder because the CSV has no double-time wage field.

What the rule says

Michigan's Prevailing Wage Act. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

Michigan LEO's rates page sets a standard overtime schedule: Sundays and recognized holidays use twice the base rate. A classification's schedule can explicitly set other rules.

The checker uses the selected weekday for day one and any marked holiday days. It can count double-time hours and show twice the entered base, but it cannot compare double-time pay because no such rate column is supplied.

Double time (2× the base rate) applies to hours worked on Sundays and recognized holidays.

A week that trips it

The worker names, trades and rates are made up and illustrative. Both result boxes below are printed from the free checker's output for the displayed CSV and rate table.

Ben C. works four weekdays and a short Sunday in this sample Michigan week. Day one is Monday, so the final daily-hours column is Sunday. No holidays are marked and four tens are off. These options are printed beside the table because the weekday mapping changes which hours the note describes.

The combined week is under the weekly threshold. Sunday still produces a reminder under the selected standard schedule. The Michigan-rules note appears alongside it, making the active mode visible rather than leaving a reader to assume the federal weekly test was used.

Michigan rules are on. Time and a half counts for hours over 8 a day, hours over 40 a week and Saturdays. Double time counts for Sundays and the holidays you marked. A trade's schedule can set other rules.

Michigan double time

Ben C., Carpenter: 4 hours on a Sunday or a holiday you marked. Michigan's standard rule is double time, at least $60.00 an hour before fringe. This page can't see your double-time rate, so check it by hand.

The fix

For the original week, review the actual Sunday payment by hand. Twice the sample base is a $60.00 hourly wage before fringe. The 4 Sunday hours use that standard schedule, plus the unchanged hourly fringe. The tool cannot verify that payment or measure a gap.

The corrected comparison is a future schedule with those four hours worked on Friday instead. Total hours remain 36, and no day exceeds eight hours. Michigan mode stays on. This removes the Sunday condition and its note, but it does not repair a past Sunday payment. Never move hours to a different date unless the work record supports it.

What the checker doesn't check here

The page does not read the trade's schedule or its explicit overtime exceptions. It cannot see a double-time wage, start and end times, registration status or the state payroll submission deadline.

Turning Michigan mode off would hide the schedule rather than resolve it. MCL 408.1102(1) exempts contracts that require federal prevailing wages from Michigan's act, so project coverage needs a separate review.

Questions about this check

Does a trade's schedule ever set different overtime terms?

Yes. LEO's rates page says its standard schedule applies unless a classification explicitly notes other overtime rules. The checker does not read those schedules.

Can the checker confirm the double-time amount paid?

No. Double-time hours get a note because the CSV has no double-time rate column.

Is Michigan's act applied to every federal prevailing wage contract?

No. MCL 408.1102(1) exempts contracts that require federal prevailing wages under 40 U.S.C. 3141 to 3148.
