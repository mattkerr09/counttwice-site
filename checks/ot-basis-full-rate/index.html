Why does Davis-Bacon overtime use the full cash rate when it includes fringe?

The checker treats rate_paid as wages and uses that full rate unless cash in place of fringe is entered in its own column.

What the rule says

Overtime. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

The checker follows the distinction in 29 CFR 5.32(c)(1) between cash in place of fringe and an ordinary straight-time cash wage. Whether a payment is in lieu of fringe is a question of fact.

The checker takes the column split as that fact. Its full-rate approach is strict and can flag a payroll that DOL's W example would allow. Moving an amount to cash_fringe is appropriate only when that is what the payment represents.

or is simply part of their straight time cash wage. In the latter situation, the cash payment is not excludable in computing overtime compensation.

A week that trips it

Ivy N. works a week with overtime and has one combined cash rate in the wage field. The first run pays enough overtime even on that full wage, so the page can show the basis note without also showing an overtime shortfall. The related wage-credit note appears as well.

These two notes answer different questions. One explains how cash above base meets the straight-time fringe total. The other explains what the checker used as overtime wages. Covering fringe through excess cash does not cause the program to silently rewrite the wage field.

The fix

The comparison records a supported $30.00 wage and $10.00 cash fringe. It changes the overtime wage from $60.00 to $50.00 because the $10.00 cash fringe is now paid separately on overtime hours too. Total cash on an overtime hour remains $60.00. One and a half times the newly separated base wage gives the minimum overtime wage, and the entered overtime wage exceeds it.

Both tables record 45 hours. The regular hourly cash total also remains $40.00. This demonstrates a different column split without pretending that reducing total pay is necessary to clear a note. Keep a combined rate when the facts do not support an in-lieu split.

What the checker doesn't check here

The CSV cannot prove why a cash amount was paid. The tool relies on the labels supplied by the contractor and cannot resolve a factual dispute over whether a payment was fringe or wages.

Other-job hours remain outside the file. Above-fringe cash in cash_fringe can count as wages under 40 U.S.C. 3142(e), so a separate column is not a way to exclude every cash dollar from overtime.

Questions about this check

Can cash in place of fringe be excluded from the basis?

29 CFR 5.32 says it can, but the payment must actually be in lieu of fringe. The checker takes the user's entered split as that fact.

Why can this be stricter than DOL's W example?

The checker's full-rate approach treats the wage field entirely as wages until an in-lieu part is separately entered.

What happens to cash fringe above the fringe still owed?

Following 40 U.S.C. 3142(e), the checker counts that extra cash as wages when calculating the overtime basis and the required overtime wage.
