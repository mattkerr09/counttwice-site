Certified payroll overtime not checked: what does a blank overtime rate mean?

A blank overtime wage means the checker reports the required rate in a note and cannot compare it with what was paid.

What the rule says

Overtime. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

40 U.S.C. 3702(a) requires at least one and a half times the basic rate of pay for overtime hours. Fringe is kept at its straight amount, and the wage determination's base remains the minimum overtime basis.

When ot_rate_paid is missing or blank, the checker gives the minimum as a note. It does not assume that a blank means zero, and it does not assign a wage shortfall without an entered overtime rate.

wages include compensation, at a rate not less than one and one-half times the basic rate of pay, for all hours worked in excess of 40 hours in the workweek.

40 U.S.C. 3702(a). Source read 8 November 2026.

in no event can the regular or basic rate upon which premium pay for overtime is calculated ... be less than the amount determined by the Secretary of Labor as the basic hourly rate (i.e. cash rate)

29 CFR 5.32(a). Source read 8 November 2026.

A week that trips it

The worker names, trades and rates are made up and illustrative. Both result boxes below are printed from the free checker's output for the displayed CSV and rate table.

Gus K. has a fraction of an hour beyond a full straight-time week. The daily hours are present and the base-plus-fringe total is covered. What is missing is the wage paid on the overtime portion. The note identifies the small amount of premium time and the minimum wage rate to compare.

This can happen when a payroll export omits an optional column. Read the payroll record before filling it in. A missing field says nothing about whether the worker was paid the minimum, more than the minimum, or less than the minimum. The check needs that fact as an input.

Overtime not checked

Gus K., Carpenter: 0.25 hours of overtime. Overtime needs at least $45.00 an hour, not counting cash fringe. Add an ot_rate_paid column to check what you paid.

The fix

Enter an overtime wage of $45.00 only if that is the rate actually paid. The recorded hours total 40.25, leaving 0.25 hours beyond 40. The $30.00 basis times one and a half gives a $45.00 required overtime wage. The same $10.00 plan credit continues on every hour.

The corrected run can now perform the comparison instead of stopping at a reminder. A quarter hour is still time worked; the tool keeps hours to hundredths. If the actual overtime rate is lower, enter it accurately and use the resulting flag to work out the correction.

What the checker doesn't check here

The result cannot recover an omitted overtime wage from gross pay because gross pay is not an input. The checker also cannot see hours on other projects in this workweek.

An apprentice or a worker spread across multiple trades follows a different path and does not get this single-row overtime comparison. The file must describe the whole available week accurately.

Questions about this check

What if the overtime rate column is missing entirely?

The checker gives a note with the minimum when the overtime wage is not in the file.

Is the required overtime basis allowed below base?

No. 29 CFR 5.32(a) sets the wage determination's basic hourly rate as the floor for the overtime basis.

Is cash paid in place of fringe part of overtime wages?

29 CFR 5.32 excludes the in-lieu amount from the basis. Under 40 U.S.C. 3142(e), cash fringe beyond the fringe still owed counts as wages.
