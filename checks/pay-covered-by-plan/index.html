Davis-Bacon wage below base but benefits cover the total: why is there a note?

The checker gives a note when cash and plan payments cover base plus fringe even though the wage field is below base.

What the rule says

Base rate plus fringe. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

40 U.S.C. 3142(d) sets an aggregate test. Under it, the combined cash payments, contributions and costs are compared with the required base and fringe total.

The checker therefore does not report a straight-time shortfall solely because rate_paid is below base. It still uses at least the wage determination's base as the overtime floor, as 29 CFR 5.32(a) requires.

may be discharged by making payments in cash, by making contributions ..., or by any combination ..., where the aggregate of the payments, contributions, and costs is not less than the basic hourly rate of pay plus the amount referred to in section 3141(2)(B)

40 U.S.C. 3142(d). Source read 8 November 2026.

A week that trips it

The worker names, trades and rates are made up and illustrative. Both result boxes below are printed from the free checker's output for the displayed CSV and rate table.

Ana R. has a cash wage below the illustrative Carpenter base and a larger hourly plan credit. These are distinct inputs. Adding them explains the note: the combined amount reaches the sample obligation even though the cash wage column is lower than the base column.

This is a note, not an instruction to cut anyone's cash pay. There is no overtime in the example, and the result assigns no weekly shortfall. Keeping that distinction clear matters when a reviewer sees the word under in the title and expects a missing payment.

Notes

Worth a look. Not counted as a shortfall.

Wage under the base rate, total covered

Ana R., Carpenter: the $28.00 wage is under the $30.00 base rate, but cash and plan payments of $40.00 cover base plus fringe of $40.00. The law counts the total. Overtime is still figured on at least $30.00.

The fix

The comparison uses a $30.00 wage and a $10.00 plan credit instead of $28.00 and $12.00. The original entered total is $40.00 an hour, matching the required $40.00. The new split adds to the same hourly total over the same hours. The second version removes the below-base condition, so this particular note disappears.

That is a different supported payment split, not a claim that a benefit can be renamed after payment. A contractor needs records for the wages and benefit credit actually used. If the original split is accurate, retain it and review the limits of the aggregate test rather than altering the file to hide a note.

What the checker doesn't check here

Other laws' minimum cash wage floors are not checked. Nor can the page decide whether the plan is bona fide, funded, annualized or subject to approval requirements. Those facts sit outside the CSV.

Benefit contribution dates are not present. 29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i) limits how contributions made less often than weekly count, but this tool cannot compare a payment schedule with that rule.

Questions about this check

Why is there no straight-time shortfall?

The law tests the total (40 U.S.C. 3142(d)). A below-base wage gets this note when cash and plan payments together cover the base-plus-fringe obligation.

May overtime use the lower cash wage as its floor?

No. 29 CFR 5.32(a) says the overtime basis can never be less than the wage determination's basic hourly rate.

Does the note settle minimum cash wage rules under other laws?

No. This page does not check minimum cash wage floors under other laws.
