What the rule says

Base rate plus fringe. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

The Davis-Bacon rules test the total paid for each hour against the wage determination's base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d) and 29 CFR 5.31(b)). Cash, plan payments or a mix can meet that total. A cash wage below the base does not by itself tell the whole story.

The checker names this flag after the low cash wage only when the combined payment is also short. A separate note covers the case where plan payments make up the difference.

may be discharged by making payments in cash, by making contributions ..., or by any combination ..., where the aggregate of the payments, contributions, and costs is not less than the basic hourly rate of pay plus the amount referred to in section 3141(2)(B)

40 U.S.C. 3142(d). Source read 8 November 2026.

and by making an additional cash payment in lieu of the required benefits

29 CFR 5.31(b). Source read 8 November 2026.

Pay under the base rate

Eli G., Laborer: paid $20.50 an hour against a $22.00 base rate. Cash and plan payments come to $26.50 an hour, and base plus fringe is $28.00. That's $1.50 an hour short, about $60.00 this week.

The fix

Raise Eli's cash wage from $20.50 to $22.00 and keep $6.00 in cash fringe. The original total is $26.50 against $28.00 due. The difference is $1.50 for each of 40 hours, or $60.00. The second table records the higher wage and the result below it comes from a fresh run.

For a real payroll, check the work records and the amount actually paid before editing an export. Changing a CSV alone does not pay a worker. Use the comparison to trace the gap back to the payroll entry, then keep the records that support the correction.

What the checker doesn't check here

The plan credit is accepted as typed. This check cannot tell whether benefits qualify or whether their hourly value was annualized. Minimum cash wage floors under other laws are outside this calculation.

The typed trade and rate table are inputs, not findings. The checker does not choose a wage determination, verify the work classification, or review deductions and net pay.

Questions about this check

Does a low cash wage always create this flag?

No. The test uses cash wages, cash fringe and plan payments together. If the total covers base plus fringe, the checker gives a note instead of this shortfall flag.

Can cash above the base count toward fringe?

Yes. Under 29 CFR 5.31(b), cash above the basic hourly rate can count toward the fringe obligation.

If plan payments make up for a low cash wage, does the overtime floor drop?

No. 29 CFR 5.32(a) says the overtime basis can never be lower than the wage determination's basic hourly rate.
