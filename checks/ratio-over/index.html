What the rule says

Apprentices. A dated summary, not legal advice. Sources read 8 November 2026. Example checked 10 November 2026.

29 CFR 5.5(a)(4)(i)(C) says the permitted apprentice ratio comes from the registered program or the ratio applicable to the project locality. An apprentice above the permitted ratio must receive the full wage determination rate for the work.

The checker counts only workers present in the file. For each day it multiplies the journeyworker count by the entered A:J ratio and keeps the whole-number part. It cannot identify which apprentice is the extra one.

In addition, any apprentice performing work on the job site in excess of the ratio permitted under this section must be paid not less than the applicable wage rate on the wage determination for the work actually performed.

29 CFR 5.5(a)(4)(i)(C). Source read 8 November 2026.

A week that trips it

The worker names, trades and rates are made up and illustrative. Both result boxes below are printed from the free checker's output for the displayed CSV and rate table.

Fay H. works as an apprentice on the last day of this sample week while the only journeyworker is absent. The typed ratio is one apprentice for one journeyworker. The other days have both workers, so the flag points to the single day with no journeyworker rather than treating the whole week alike.

Rates for apprentice wages appear in the CSV so the example resembles an export, but the checker does not compare those wages with program steps. A ratio problem produces a flag about staffing and a full-rate reminder, not a guessed dollar amount. The apprentice-pay note also remains visible.

The fix

The corrected comparison is a staffing change for a planned week. Ana R. also works eight hours on day five, giving one journeyworker and one apprentice that day. One times the entered 1:1 ratio permits one apprentice. On the first four days the same count was already within the limit.

For a week already worked, adding a person who was absent would make the record false. Use actual attendance and resolve any excess-apprentice pay under 29 CFR 5.5(a)(4)(i)(C). The comparison only shows why real journeyworker presence removes the ratio flag. The separate apprentice-pay note persists because program wages still need a review outside this tool.

What the checker doesn't check here

Only this file's workers count, even though a program ratio can concern the entire workforce. The tool does not check registration, local program terms, graduated ratios or apprentice wage steps.

No dollar figure is assigned to the excess count. The page cannot choose the apprentice who must receive the full rate, and adding a ratio does not prove the program permits it.

Questions about this check

Where should the entered ratio come from?

29 CFR 5.5(a)(4)(i)(C) and (D) point to the registered program and the ratio applicable to the project locality, including locality rules for out-of-area programs.

What happens to an apprentice above the ratio?

29 CFR 5.5(a)(4)(i)(C) says an apprentice working above the permitted ratio must be paid at least the applicable wage determination rate for the work performed.

Does a passing count establish registration?

No. The page does not check individual apprentice or program registration.
