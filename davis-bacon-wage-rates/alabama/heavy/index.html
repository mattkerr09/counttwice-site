What these rates show

Electrical work shows the largest cash spread among Alabama's core heavy trades. AL20260063 lists $15.24 plus $2.50 fringe as a survey rate from a decision covering Covington County. The union rate in AL20260121, a decision covering Greene County, is $31.00 plus $16.22. Both the wage and benefit allowance change substantially between these sources. Choose the heavy decision for the project before setting the electrical portion of the bid.

Carpentry is another place where the survey figure will not cover the upper union obligation. The lower line in AL20260121, a decision covering Greene County, has $14.60 base and $0.00 fringe. AL20260108, a decision covering Lawrence County, gives the higher carpenter line as $30.26 plus $14.78. The difference extends beyond hourly cash. When the crew moves between heavy projects, retain the decision with the estimate so these two obligations do not get mixed.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
