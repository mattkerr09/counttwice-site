What these rates show

Alabama residential concrete rates span more than two to one in the published survey lines. AL20260043, a decision covering Geneva County, lists a cement mason base of $8.001 with $0.00 fringe. AL20260056, a decision covering Russell County, gives $18.00 plus $0.00. The upper cash requirement is much higher even though both fringes are zero. A statewide concrete allowance would hide that difference before the first labor hour is priced.

Roofing shows that a low residential base and a zero fringe are separate facts to check. The lower survey roofer line is $7.252 plus $0.00 in AL20260018, a decision covering De Kalb County. The upper line in AL20260053, a decision covering Mobile County, carries $13.98 and $0.81. Here the fringe is no longer zero at the higher base. Keep that small benefit amount in a roof estimate rather than assuming every survey roofing line has none.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
