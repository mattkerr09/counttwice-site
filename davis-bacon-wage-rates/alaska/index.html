What these rates show

Alaska's combined page includes different construction schedules, so the roof range is a comparison across project types. The lower survey line in AK20260004, a decision covering Anchorage, is $13.131 with $0.00 fringe. The union roof line in AK20260001, a decision covering Aleutians East and 25 other counties, pays $52.07 plus $19.62. Those two ends are far apart. Establish whether the contract calls for residential, building or another schedule before using this range to cost the roof.

Electrical figures also reflect that schedule split. AK20260005, a decision covering Aleutians East and 17 other counties, has a survey base of $17.45 and $3.18 fringe, while AK20260001, a decision covering Aleutians East and 25 other counties, prints a union base of $55.44 plus $30.33. The benefit increase is substantial alongside the cash increase. Several Alaska decisions cover many named areas. The first place in a coverage list cannot tell you where an individual electrical line applies without reading its work and area limits.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
