What these rates show

American Samoa has one decision for building, heavy, highway and residential projects in this source set. Its carpenter line lists $6.30 per hour with $0.00 fringe in AS20260001, a decision covering Eastern and 4 other counties. There is no separate low and high carpenter base to compare between decisions. That makes the construction inventory simple, but the printed classification still matters when deciding whether the crew's actual work belongs on this line or on another line in the decision.

The electrician entry in AS20260001 also gives $6.30 base and $0.00 fringe, from a decision covering Eastern and 4 other counties. Matching dollar amounts do not make carpentry and electrical work one classification. Keep each worker's duties and hours under the appropriate trade when preparing payroll. The table repeats the available limits so the absence of a range is clear. For work outside the common trades shown here, use the full American Samoa schedule rather than extending an electrician amount to an unrelated job.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
