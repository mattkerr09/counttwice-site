What these rates show

Arizona heavy electrical work has a wide split between survey and union rates. AZ20260006, a decision covering Maricopa and 4 other counties, supplies a survey base of $16.00 with $2.84 fringe. The union line in AZ20260051, a decision covering Gila County, is $46.56 plus $19.15. The union base is close to three times the survey base. This comparison belongs to heavy work, so choose the contract's heavy schedule before assigning an electrical rate to the crew.

The general laborer comparison is much closer than the electrical one. In AZ20260062, a decision covering Yuma County, the survey line pays $21.54 with $0.00 fringe. AZ20260051, a decision covering Gila County, sets the union base at $27.41 plus $9.26. A zero fringe at the survey end does not carry over to the union end.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
