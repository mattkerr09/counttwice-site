What these rates show

Arizona's highway general laborer limits both come from survey lines. The lower is $22.35 plus $0.60 fringe in AZ20260046, a decision covering Mohave County; the upper is $32.35 plus $6.98 in AZ20260033, a decision covering Navajo County. Survey does not mean one price across the state. Both cash and benefits change here. Use the line for the project rather than carrying a highway labor allowance from an earlier bid in another part of Arizona.

Carpentry brings union rates into the highway comparison. AZ20260035, a decision covering Cochise County, prints the survey carpenter line at $28.08 with $12.74 fringe. AZ20260044, a decision covering Coconino County, has the union line at $35.89 plus $14.98. Unlike the laborer comparison, the carpenter fringes at these two ends are fairly close. The larger change is in base pay. Named equipment and truck groups below remain separate work descriptions, not substitutes for the carpenter classification.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
