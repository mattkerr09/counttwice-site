What these rates show

Residential concrete work in Arizona includes both a low survey line and a union line with a sizeable fringe. The survey cement mason in AZ20260005, a decision covering Apache and 2 other counties, is $8.961 plus $0.00. The union line in AZ20260001, a decision covering Maricopa County, pays $28.78 with $9.17. This is a wider gap than a modest local wage adjustment. Resolve the residential source before using a flat labor rate to cost the slab or other concrete work.

Carpentry shows a different pattern: the range ends are both survey rates, and both have zero fringe. AZ20260005, a decision covering Apache and 2 other counties, lists $10.001 plus $0.00; AZ20260002, a decision covering Pima County, lists $24.45 plus $0.00. The high base is more than twice the low one despite the shared source kind. Arizona's residential wage differences therefore cannot be explained by union versus survey alone. Check the county coverage even when a benefit column looks the same.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
