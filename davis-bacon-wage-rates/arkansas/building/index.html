What these rates show

Arkansas building carpentry moves from a survey base of $14.18 with $0.00 fringe in AR20260005, a decision covering Greene County, to a union base of $31.93 plus $11.83 in AR20260034, a decision covering Sebastian County. The upper cash rate is more than twice the lower one, before benefits are added. Establish the building decision at the start of the bid so a carpenter allowance from a lower survey schedule does not become the default for every project.

Plumbing carries a similar cash gap, with benefits adding another large difference. AR20260011, a decision covering Pope County, gives the survey plumber $14.54 plus $1.08. The union line at AR20260004, a decision covering Drew County, requires $34.41 and $15.70. The fringe beside the high plumber base is greater than the one beside the high carpenter base. Those separate trade obligations matter when comparing crew costs. Do not move the carpenter benefit allowance onto a plumbing line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
