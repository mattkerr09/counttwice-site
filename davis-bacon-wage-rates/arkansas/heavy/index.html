What these rates show

Heavy electrical work in Arkansas has a survey low and a union high, with benefits due at both ends. The line in AR20260052, a decision covering Faulkner County, is $22.48 plus $7.92 fringe. At AR20260056, a decision covering Pulaski County, the union obligation is $23.22 base with $9.75. The higher fringe is a material part of the difference. Include it when testing whether an electrical allowance covers the wage obligation for the heavy contract.

Concrete rates tell a less dramatic story than electrical rates on these heavy schedules. The cement mason survey line in AR20260058, a decision covering Washington County, gives $14.36 plus $0.00, compared with $18.41 plus $3.46 in AR20260053, a decision covering Garland County. Both ends are survey figures, but only the lower end has zero fringe. The table separates cash and benefits so this difference remains visible without treating every Arkansas heavy trade as if it followed the electrical spread.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
