What these rates show

Arkansas highway laborer rates here are survey figures, with no hourly dollar fringe at either end of the general range. AR20260136, a decision covering Drew County, shows $10.081 plus $0.00, while AR20260129, a decision covering Baxter County, shows $18.67 plus $0.00. The cash difference is still large enough to change a road bid with many labor hours.

The carpenter range also comes from survey lines with zero fringes at its ends. AR20260174, a decision covering Little River and 1 other counties, lists $12.462 and $0.00; AR20260160, a decision covering Craighead County, lists $20.23 and $0.00. Here the spread is smaller than for general laborers, and the low source covers more than one county. A broad decision's first county is not a location label for its carpenter rate. The coverage wording below preserves that distinction while linking the original work description. Road equipment examples include pavers for asphalt, aggregate and concrete, plus a roller description. The truck details name water trucks and flatbed trucks. Check those paving and hauling duties before extending crew hours in an Arkansas road estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
