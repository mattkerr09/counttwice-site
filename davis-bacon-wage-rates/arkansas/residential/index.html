What these rates show

Arkansas residential concrete work spans a survey line and a union line with a large benefit requirement. AR20260063, a decision covering Arkansas County, gives $12.201 base plus $0.00 fringe. The union cement mason in AR20260109, a decision covering Benton County, requires $35.10 plus $18.78. The higher cash rate is almost three times the lower one. For a concrete bid, checking the residential decision changes the labor allowance much more than a small adjustment to an assumed statewide rate.

Roofing has a wide cash range without a fringe difference at these ends. The survey roofer in AR20260103, a decision covering Woodruff County, pays $7.532 plus $0.00; the upper survey line in AR20260114, a decision covering Faulkner County, pays $19.23 with $0.00. That is a different cost pattern from concrete. A roof estimate needs the local cash requirement even when both source lines show zero benefits. Neither of these survey roofing figures can stand in for the union concrete obligation above.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
