Reading the summaries

Choose the state and then the construction type named in the contract. American Samoa, Guam, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands each have one page for all available types. Places with fewer than ten decisions also have one combined page. Types also share a page when at least 90% of the smaller decision set appears in the other type. The decision list on each combined page tells you which types each document covers.

Read the county coverage beside the amount. Some decisions cover several places, while the work description can narrow where a rate applies. A state range helps you compare costs across decisions. Your bid still needs the rate for the actual location, duties and contract modification.

Carry the fringe beside the chosen hourly base into your estimate. A table can show low and high fringe amounts from different decisions. Open the source link to find the fringe that goes with your chosen base. Dollar amounts, percentages and written terms appear separately so you can read the full obligation.

General trade rates appear in the main columns. Restricted work, such as low-voltage wiring or drywall installation, has a separate line with its own quoted description. Equipment groups and truck duties also keep their own lines. Open the specialty list under the trade to see those figures.

Union and survey rates can appear together in one state. Each source link names the decision and the rate line it came from. Publication dates show when the decision changed. Dates beside the trade rates tell you when those rates took effect. Check both against the document in the contract.

For payroll, use the classification for the duties performed. Keep time records when workers cross between trades. The table helps locate published amounts. The full decision supplies the conditions you need to read before assigning a rate to an hour of covered work.
