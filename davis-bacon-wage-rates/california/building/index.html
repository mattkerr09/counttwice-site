What these rates show

California building bids can cross a very large survey-to-union gap. Carpentry in CA20260016, a decision covering Tulare County, starts at a survey base of $13.361 plus $4.24 fringe. The union carpenter line for San Francisco County in CA20260018 pays $60.39 with $33.52. The higher base is more than four times the lower one. Read the area printed beside the trade, since California decisions can contain several county-specific rates under the same broad coverage list.

Electrical work widens the gap further. CA20260023, a decision covering Amador County, has the survey electrician at $16.30 plus $3.87. The union line for San Francisco County in CA20260018 is $91.25 plus $48.05. That high fringe is substantial even before it is added to cash wages. The building equipment details group cranes, piledriving and hoisting separately from other power equipment work. For a building crew that uses lifting equipment, carry that full group description into the estimate with its rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
