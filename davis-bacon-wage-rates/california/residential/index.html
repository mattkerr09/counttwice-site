What these rates show

The California residential set contains survey electrical pay far below its upper union lines. CA20260011, a decision covering Kings County, lists $7.731 base with $0.00 fringe. The union electrician line for San Francisco County: in CA20260019 requires $91.25 plus $48.05. A residential label by itself therefore says very little about the electrical cost. The decision's construction coverage and the county note on the line both need to match the job before the amount enters the bid.

Residential plumbing costs vary sharply between California's survey and union sources. The survey line in CA20260011, a decision covering Kings County, is $8.251 plus $0.00; the union plumber line in CA20260019 pays $91.00 plus $49.49 for Marin, San Francisco & Sonoma counties. Its listed county group is the rate's location evidence. The decision may cover other places too. A plumber figure from this residential set should not be carried into a heavy or highway bid without its own schedule review.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
