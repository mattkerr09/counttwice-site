What these rates show

Colorado building carpentry has a survey low with a small fringe and a union high with a larger one. CO20260001, a decision covering Alamosa and 46 other counties, gives $15.94 plus $3.41. CO20260015, a decision covering Adams County, gives $35.09 plus $13.84. The upper cash rate is more than twice the lower rate. Some of these building decisions cover many counties, so their coverage lists help locate a source but cannot replace a narrower trade-specific county or zone note.

Electrician rates show why those narrower notes matter. The survey line at CO20260025, a decision covering Mesa County, is $25.63 plus $9.51. The upper union line is $49.35 plus $19.93 in CO20260001 for a decision covering Alamosa and 46 other counties. That long county list belongs to the electrical line itself. It should stay with the quoted amount when the bid file is prepared. Using the decision's first county instead would erase the line's stated reach and could point an estimator to the wrong place.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
