What these rates show

The general carpenter base stays level across the Connecticut building sources included here. CT20260019, a decision covering Litchfield County, supplies the first union line at $42.03 plus $29.19 fringe. A flat base range does not mean every carpenter duty has the same price. Drywall and other named specialty work sit separately in the table. Check the full carpenter description before assuming this common base covers all of the interior and structural tasks in the building bid.

Concrete work has a much wider spread than the general carpenter base. The survey cement mason at CT20260019, a decision covering Litchfield County, receives $29.36 with $0.00 fringe. The union line in CT20260020, a decision covering Fairfield County, requires $45.56 plus $37.97. At the upper end, benefits are a large addition to cash wages. Connecticut's level carpenter base therefore should not lead an estimator to expect a level concrete cost or a similar fringe obligation across these building decisions.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
