What these rates show

Connecticut heavy concrete work has a large survey-to-union difference in both base and fringe. CT20260015, a decision covering Middlesex and 1 other counties, shows $25.52 plus $8.49. The union cement mason at CT20260013, a decision covering Fairfield County, is $46.09 plus $36.29. Benefits account for much of the change in total labor cost. For a heavy concrete allowance, checking only which source has the higher hourly cash figure would leave out a substantial part of what the contract may require.

General laborers also have lower survey and higher union lines in this heavy set. CT20260014, a decision covering Hartford County, lists $21.03 base with $5.30 fringe. CT20260013, a decision covering Fairfield County, lists $35.70 with $28.85. The upper fringe is large relative to its base. Keep the laborer and concrete classifications separate even where their county coverage overlaps.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
