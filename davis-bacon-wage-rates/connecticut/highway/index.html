What these rates show

Connecticut highway electrical rates have more than one union base within a single decision. CT20260001, Fairfield County (Darien, Greenwich, New Canaan, Stamford), gives a lower electrician line of $45.00 plus $36.63 fringe. Its upper line in CT20260001, Middlesex County (Cromwell, Middlefield, Middleton and Portland); Tolland County; Windham County, gives $48.25 plus $36.67. County coverage alone cannot distinguish those amounts because the document covers the same broad area. Read the named electrical work on each line before deciding which base belongs in the road estimate.

The general laborer range is flat by comparison. The union line in CT20260001, a decision covering Fairfield and 5 other counties, carries $35.70 base and $28.85 fringe. That benefit obligation is substantial even without a low-to-high cash spread. A single base in this table means the included general lines do not produce a wider range; it does not price every laborer duty. Read the separate specialty classifications in the highway determination when the crew's tasks go beyond the general description.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
