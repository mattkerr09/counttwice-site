What these rates show

Connecticut residential plumbing can cost very different amounts under the survey and union schedules. The lower line is $14.25 plus $2.31 fringe in CT20260004, a decision covering Litchfield and 1 other counties. The upper union line in CT20260007, a decision covering Fairfield County, requires $50.58 and $35.85. Both components rise sharply. Establish the residential decision in the contract before extending plumbing hours, rather than assuming a residential project always uses the lower survey cash rate.

The electrical range also crosses survey and union sources. CT20260008, a decision covering Hartford County, prints a survey base of $19.99 with $2.00 fringe; CT20260007, a decision covering Fairfield County, has a union base of $47.40 plus $36.74. The higher electrical fringe is almost as large as the higher cash base. That relationship makes benefits central to the estimate. Use the fringe paired with the chosen electrical rate, rather than a benefit assumption carried from the lower residential source.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
