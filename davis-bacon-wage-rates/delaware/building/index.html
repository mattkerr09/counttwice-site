What these rates show

Delaware's building carpenter comparison stays within union rates. DE20260009, a decision covering Sussex County, lists $32.87 plus $18.98 fringe, while DE20260007, a decision covering Kent County, gives $38.27 plus $25.34. Both pay components rise at the upper end, so the base difference understates the change in total labor cost. The state has only a few county areas, but that does not make a single carpenter allowance fit every building decision shown in this summary.

Plumbing gives a useful contrast to carpentry because both plumber limits are survey lines. DE20260007, a decision covering Kent County, has $45.40 base and $16.23 fringe. DE20260008, a decision covering New Castle County, has $47.73 plus $18.40. The cash spread is small compared with the carpenter spread, and the fringes are sizeable at both ends. A survey source should not be read as a zero-benefit source. Match the plumbing line itself before adding the labor allowance to a Delaware building bid.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
