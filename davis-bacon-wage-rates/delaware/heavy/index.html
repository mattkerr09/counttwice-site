What these rates show

Delaware heavy general laborer rates show a survey low and a union high with a particularly large fringe difference. DE20260012, a decision covering Sussex County, pays $16.42 plus $4.06; DE20260011, a decision covering New Castle County, pays $29.45 plus $25.70. The upper fringe approaches the upper base. That changes the cost of labor-heavy work much more than the cash spread alone suggests. Keep the relevant county decision with the crew estimate before comparing heavy bids.

Electrical base pay is much closer across these heavy survey lines. The lower cash line in DE20260011, a decision covering New Castle County, has $34.42 plus $24.02 fringe. The higher cash line in DE20260010, a decision covering Kent County, has $34.96 plus $23.87. Its fringe is slightly lower, so selecting the highest base and highest fringe separately would combine two different obligations. The nearly level electrical cash range should not be mistaken for the much wider general laborer cost pattern above.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
