What these rates show

Delaware highway carpentry has a noticeable base spread even though both limits are survey rates. DE20260006, a decision covering Sussex County, lists $39.67 plus $5.08 fringe. DE20260004, a decision covering Kent County, lists $50.70 plus $3.74. The upper cash line has a smaller fringe than the lower one. A road bid needs each pair intact. Treating the largest fringe as an automatic addition to the largest base would create a carpenter figure that neither of these lines prints.

Cement masons follow the same direction in cash but have their own benefit amounts. The survey line in DE20260006, a decision covering Sussex County, is $30.41 plus $3.28; the upper survey line in DE20260004, a decision covering Kent County, is $34.85 plus $1.34. Here too, the fringe falls as the quoted base rises. These highway comparisons are local schedule differences, not a union-to-survey divide. Read the concrete and carpenter lines separately when costing crews on the same road contract.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
