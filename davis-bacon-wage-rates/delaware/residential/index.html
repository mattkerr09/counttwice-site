What these rates show

Delaware's residential electrical rates come from one decision with county groups on the individual lines. DE20260001 prints $9.121 plus $0.00 fringe for New Castle County. Its higher electrician line is $10.001 plus $0.00 for Kent and Sussex counties. Both are survey rates. The document number alone cannot select between them, and its first county is not a label for every rate. The line's own county group is what gives this comparison its meaning.

Plumbing reverses the county pattern seen in the electrical lines. DE20260001 gives the lower survey plumber base, $8.921 plus $0.00, for Kent and Sussex counties. The upper plumber line at DE20260001 is $9.801 plus $0.00 for New Castle County. Zero fringe at both ends leaves the cash difference as the main comparison. A residential estimator cannot use the county group with the cheaper electrical line to assume that the plumbing line will also be cheaper there.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
