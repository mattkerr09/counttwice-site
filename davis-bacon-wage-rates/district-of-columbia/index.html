What these rates show

The District of Columbia's page combines building, residential, heavy and highway coverage from separate decisions. The survey electrical line in DC20260003, a decision covering Washington and 1 other counties, pays $19.93 plus $3.11 fringe. The union line in DC20260001, a decision covering Washington and 1 other counties, pays $59.50 plus $23.06. This is a comparison across schedules for the same jurisdiction. The project address does not resolve that split. Start with the construction type assigned to the contract before choosing an electrician rate.

Plumber rates also change between the District's schedules, even though both ends here are union lines. DC20260003, a decision covering Washington and 1 other counties, gives $29.60 base with $14.71 fringe; DC20260001, a decision covering Washington and 1 other counties, gives $53.30 with $23.71. Both cash and benefits are higher in the upper line. With no county boundary to explain the difference, the construction schedule and printed duties deserve particular attention. Do not treat this combined range as a choice of rates for one District project.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
