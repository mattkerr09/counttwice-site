What these rates show

Florida building carpentry crosses a survey-to-union gap that more than doubles the cash base. The survey line in FL20260017, a decision covering Jackson County, is $12.291 plus $0.27 fringe. FL20260210, a decision covering Lake County, lists the union carpenter at $28.65 plus $13.80. The benefit change adds to the base change. A carpenter allowance taken from one Florida building decision can therefore miss a large part of the wage obligation in another decision.

Plumbing has an even wider building cash spread. FL20260007, a decision covering Calhoun County, lists a survey base of $10.482 with $2.06 fringe, against a union base of $41.60 plus $22.68 in FL20260232, a decision covering Flagler and 1 other counties. The high cash figure is almost four times the low figure. These sources also show why a county group needs careful wording: broad decision coverage does not prove that every plumber line applies uniformly across all counties in that document.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
