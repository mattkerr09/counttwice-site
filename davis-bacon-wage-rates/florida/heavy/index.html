What these rates show

Florida heavy electrical work ranges from survey pay to union pay with a larger benefit requirement. FL20260106, a decision covering Brevard County, has $16.71 base and $3.51 fringe. FL20260107, a decision covering Broward County, gives the union electrician $40.25 plus $15.20. The upper cash figure is more than twice the lower one. Price electrical hours from the heavy decision for the project, since a general Florida labor allowance would conceal this trade's large source-dependent difference.

General laborer figures remain survey rates at both heavy limits. The line in FL20260095, a decision covering Jackson County, is $8.061 plus $0.67 fringe; the line in FL20260125, a decision covering Miami-Dade County, is $13.092 plus $1.26. The cash spread is far smaller than the electrician spread, and both fringes are modest. That contrast matters when a heavy estimate contains very different mixes of electrical and general labor hours. One percentage increase for every trade would not describe these source lines well.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
