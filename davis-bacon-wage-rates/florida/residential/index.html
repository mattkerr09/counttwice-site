What these rates show

Florida residential electrical work includes a low survey figure and a union figure with much higher cash and fringe. FL20260054, a decision covering Bay County, has $10.461 plus $0.00. FL20260056, a decision covering Broward County, gives $40.25 plus $15.20. The higher base is close to four times the lower one. A residential project label cannot tell an estimator which of these cost levels applies. The contract decision and electrical duties have to settle that question before the crew is priced.

The plumber comparison also moves from a zero-fringe survey line to a union line with benefits. FL20260088, a decision covering Volusia County, lists $11.372 base and $0.00 fringe. The union amount in FL20260053, a decision covering Baker County, is $37.50 with $17.59. Its upper base is below the electrical upper base, but that does not make the fringe interchangeable. Each trade keeps its own paired obligation. Use the residential line for the actual work rather than borrowing the building rate from a nearby Florida job.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
