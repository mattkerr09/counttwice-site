What these rates show

Georgia building carpentry shows a sizeable spread within survey rates alone. GA20260084, a decision covering Baldwin County, lists $13.301 plus $0.83 fringe, while GA20260311, a decision covering Richmond County, lists $24.83 plus $0.00. The upper cash line has zero fringe even though the lower line has a small fringe. County and classification differences therefore matter without a union-to-survey change. Keep the two pay components together when comparing building carpenter allowances across Georgia decisions.

Plumbing has a different source pattern: its low is survey and its high is union. GA20260089, a decision covering Decatur County, prints $15.00 with $0.01 fringe; GA20260094, a decision covering Jefferson County, prints $41.18 with $17.01. That upper fringe adds a sizeable obligation to a base already far above the lower line. The carpenter comparison alone would miss this union plumbing cost. Read each trade's source kind and county coverage before applying a common wage assumption to the building crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
