What these rates show

Georgia heavy electrician limits combine a survey line and a union line whose own county note is important. GA20260286, a decision covering Chatham County, has $23.67 plus $8.02 fringe. The union line in GA20260060 gives $38.65 plus $12.75 for Clay & Quitman counties. That county group belongs to the electrical rate, rather than being inferred from the decision's first county. Keep it in the bid notes so a broader decision coverage list does not obscure the line's local restriction.

Concrete pay in the heavy set has survey figures at both ends. The lower cement mason line at GA20260055, a decision covering Crisp County, is $14.11 with $1.78 fringe. GA20260286, a decision covering Chatham County, gives $21.40 with $4.25. Benefits rise alongside cash, but the spread is smaller than for electricians. A heavy bid with many concrete hours should use this concrete comparison on its own terms. Source kinds vary by trade in the Georgia heavy schedules.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
