What these rates show

Georgia highway carpenter bases differ by more than two to one between these survey lines. GA20260177, a decision covering Franklin County, gives $14.64 plus $1.98 fringe. The upper base in GA20260001, a decision covering Fulton County, is $32.68 with $0.00. The high cash line has no fringe, so the total gap is not quite the same as the base gap. Cost the carpenter item from its highway decision before multiplying a labor allowance through the road quantities.

Cement mason bases also more than double across the Georgia highway survey comparison. GA20260174, a decision covering Dodge County, has $13.071 plus $0.00; GA20260001, a decision covering Fulton County, has $30.37 plus $0.00. Both fringes are zero here. Unlike carpentry, there is no fringe offset between these two ends. Keep the concrete line separate in the estimate so a lower survey source does not become a default for every road structure or paving task classified as cement mason work.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
