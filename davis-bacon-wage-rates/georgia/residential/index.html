What these rates show

Georgia residential electrical rates stretch from survey pay to union pay with a much larger benefit amount. GA20260028, a decision covering Bryan County, has $10.421 base and $2.93 fringe. The union electrical line in GA20260040, a decision covering Hall County, requires $38.65 plus $12.75. The high base is more than three times the low one. Establish the residential determination before comparing labor bids, since the work type alone does not indicate which cash and benefit level belongs to the project.

Carpentry also has a survey low and union high, but its low fringe is zero. The line at GA20260015, a decision covering Upson County, is $9.252 with $0.00 fringe. GA20260043, a decision covering McIntosh County, gives the union carpenter $28.65 plus $12.56. Both the base increase and the new benefit obligation change the crew cost. That pattern differs from Georgia highway carpentry, so keep a residential source with the residential estimate rather than carrying a rate between work types.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
