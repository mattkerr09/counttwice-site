What these rates show

Guam's summary combines several construction schedules for the same named area. The lower survey cement mason line in GU20260001, a decision covering Guam, pays $14.92 plus $0.00 fringe. GU20260003, a decision covering Guam, has a higher survey line at $19.20 plus $0.00. This is a comparison across decisions and construction schedules, not a county choice. Geography will not select a rate on Guam. Read the construction types and the contract's decision before setting the concrete allowance.

Plumbing also varies between Guam's survey schedules while keeping zero fringe at these limits. GU20260001, a decision covering Guam, gives $16.52 base with $0.00 fringe; GU20260003, a decision covering Guam, gives $20.00 with $0.00. The cash range is slightly smaller than the cement mason range. A single island-wide plumber figure would hide that schedule difference. The decision list lets an estimator find each document, but the project still needs its assigned construction schedule and the duties printed beside the plumbing rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
