What these rates show

Hawaii has one decision covering all four construction types in this source set. The union electrical lines within HI20260001, a decision covering Hawaii and 4 other counties, range from $56.55 plus $33.16 fringe to $63.90 plus $33.38. The upper line names cable splicers; the lower line names electricians. This is a difference in printed electrical duties inside one decision. A single source number does not mean that every electrical task carries one base and one fringe amount.

The Hawaii cement mason bases are much closer together than the electrical bases. The union lines in HI20260001, a decision covering Hawaii and 4 other counties, show $44.12 and $44.27, with $33.63 fringe beside the lower base. Those small cash differences still belong to distinct printed work lines. Open the full descriptions before deciding which one covers the concrete task. The table also keeps named equipment and other specialty work separate, since the decision covers more duties than a short common-trade summary can list.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
