What these rates show

Idaho building carpentry has a large survey-to-union split. ID20260034, a decision covering Power County, gives $18.00 plus $0.00 fringe; ID20260107, a decision covering Kootenai and 1 other counties, gives $44.98 plus $17.84. The union base is well over twice the survey base, with benefits adding to that difference. A broad county list in the upper decision should not be turned into a claim that every carpenter line applies identically everywhere it covers. Read any narrower work and area notes before extending the crew hours.

Concrete work brings a zone rule into the Idaho building comparison. The survey cement mason in ID20260034, a decision covering Power County, has $13.521 plus $2.80. The union line in ID20260008, marked Zone 1, has $40.89 plus $18.64. Its zone definitions describe distances from named base points in the full source. Keep those distance rules with the estimate. A zone label is not a county-wide rate, and the decision's first county cannot resolve which zone covers a concrete crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
