What these rates show

Idaho heavy concrete rates compare a lower survey line with a higher union line that has a zone restriction. ID20260039, a decision covering Blaine County, prints $17.16 plus $5.67 fringe. ID20260040 prints $40.89 plus $18.64 under Zone 1. The upper base is more than twice the lower one. For a bid using that union source, check the zone definitions and base-point distances before calculating hours. The zone shown here preserves the printed restriction without guessing a county for it.

General laborer pay also rises from survey to union in this heavy set. ID20260062, a decision covering Twin Falls County, has $16.00 base and $3.28 fringe. The union line in ID20260109, a decision covering Bannock and 1 other counties, carries $34.25 with $13.80. Both pay components matter to the difference.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
