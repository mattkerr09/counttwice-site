What these rates show

Idaho highway concrete costs depend on the source and, for the upper union line, the printed zone. The survey cement mason at ID20260090, a decision covering Ada County, is $16.00 plus $5.41 fringe. ID20260073 gives $40.89 plus $18.64 under Zone 1. A road can extend through more than one area, so keep the full zone definition and distance conditions beside the chosen rate. The county coverage of a decision is not a substitute for its zone rules.

The general laborer comparison runs from survey pay in ID20260094, a decision covering Canyon County, at $14.02 plus $4.66, to union pay in ID20260076, a decision covering Butte County, at $33.83 plus $16.50. That is a substantial increase in benefits as well as cash. It also differs from the zone-based concrete comparison above. Check the laborer's own line instead of assuming every trade on the same highway contract follows the same zone boundaries or uses the same source kind.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
