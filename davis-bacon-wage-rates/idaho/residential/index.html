What these rates show

Idaho residential plumbing in this source set has survey rates at both limits and zero fringe beside each one. ID20260003, a decision covering Benewah and 7 other counties, gives $8.501 plus $0.00; ID20260006, a decision covering Blaine and 7 other counties, gives $12.922 plus $0.00. The cash difference is narrower than the Idaho heavy union-to-survey spread. These are residential sources, however, and their modest amounts should not be carried into another construction type. Resolve the schedule before using this range to price a plumbing crew.

The residential carpenter comparison also has survey lines with zero fringes. The lower base in ID20260004, a decision covering Bingham and 7 other counties, is $7.253 with $0.00 fringe, against $10.404 plus $0.00 in ID20260002, a decision covering Adams and 4 other counties. Each source covers a county group rather than one named job site. Those groups explain which documents to review, while the full carpenter descriptions explain the covered work. The absence of a fringe difference does not remove the cash difference between the two residential schedules.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
