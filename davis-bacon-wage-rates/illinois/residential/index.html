What these rates show

Illinois residential plumbing spans a low survey obligation and a much higher union obligation. IL20260026, a decision covering Bureau and 12 other counties, gives $14.61 base with $3.17 fringe. The union line in IL20260008, DuPage (Argonne National Laboratories and Fermi National Laboratory), Grundy & Will counties, requires $60.50 plus $39.02. The higher cash figure is more than four times the lower one. Residential coverage alone cannot tell an estimator which end applies. Review the contract's county coverage and plumber duties before comparing labor costs or accepting a subcontract allowance.

The concrete spread is larger still in proportional terms. The survey cement mason at IL20260026, a decision covering Bureau and 12 other counties, is $10.691 with $2.49 fringe. IL20260009, a decision covering Cook County, has a union base of $53.10 plus $43.80. Its cash base is close to five times the lower one. These residential concrete and plumbing comparisons show why a low survey schedule cannot be treated as a statewide residential price list. Each trade needs the source assigned to the project.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
