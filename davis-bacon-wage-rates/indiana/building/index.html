What these rates show

Indiana building carpentry includes a low survey line and a much higher union line restricted to a county group. IN20260010, a decision covering Jefferson and 1 other counties, has $10.241 with $0.00 fringe. The union carpenter for Lake, LaPorte and Porter counties in IN20260001 receives $45.91 plus $35.90. Benefits make the gap wider than the large base difference alone. Check the line's county group before using the union amount, rather than assigning it to every county named elsewhere in the building decision.

Electrical cash pay has an even wider building range. The survey line at IN20260010, a decision covering Jefferson and 1 other counties, gives $10.001 plus $0.58, against $52.00 plus $32.85 for the union line at IN20260001, Lake County. The upper electrical base is more than five times the lower one. The upper carpenter and electrician lines also have different printed county scopes. A county grouping that fits the carpenter rate is not enough evidence for where the electrician amount applies.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
