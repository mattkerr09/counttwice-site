What these rates show

Indiana heavy and highway concrete rates are union lines, but the geographic limits can be more detailed than a county name. IN20260006 gives the lower cement mason $23.38 plus $11.94 for Adams, Allen, DeKalb, Huntington, Kosciusko, Lagrange, Noble, Steuben, Wells and Whitley counties. The upper line in IN20260006 is $43.72 plus $35.59, restricted by southward on Rt. No. 49 to the Jasper, Benton and White county lines, including the city limits of Wheatfield, Rensselaer and Remington, Indiana. To the west, the boundary of Newton County. Road and town boundaries form part of that printed area. Keep them with the rate rather than reducing the upper concrete quote to the first county in the decision. For a concrete takeoff, mark which part of the site falls within the printed restriction before entering the labor allowance. A town or road named by the source can matter even when the project stays inside one county. Keep the boundary description available to the person preparing payroll, so a later crew change does not turn the statewide comparison into the assigned job rate.

Plumbing has its own union spread within the heavy and highway decision. For Clark, Floyd and Harrison counties, IN20260006 prints $32.00 plus $20.13 fringe. The upper plumber line in IN20260006, Bartholomew, Boone, Hamilton, Hancock, Hendricks, Howard, Johnson and Marion counties; Miami County (south of a straight line where Route 218 enters W. Boundary); Morgan, Shelby and Tipton counties, carries $50.05 with $20.39. The cash difference is large while these two fringe amounts are close. That differs from the concrete comparison, where benefits rise sharply. A heavy and highway estimate needs each trade's actual pair of amounts and area restrictions, even when both trades share a decision number.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
