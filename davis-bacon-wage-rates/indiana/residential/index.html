What these rates show

Indiana residential general laborer rates include both survey and union sources. IN20260032, a decision covering Jackson County, gives $13.93 plus $0.00 fringe. The upper union line in IN20260051, a decision covering Elkhart and 2 other counties, requires $37.34 plus $26.03. The upper base is well above twice the lower base, with a large benefit obligation added.

Carpenter cash pay also more than doubles between the residential survey and union limits. IN20260019, a decision covering Wayne County, lists $16.00 base with $0.51 fringe; IN20260054, a decision covering Jasper and 3 other counties, lists $35.08 with $18.50. The high carpenter fringe is lower than the high general laborer fringe in the comparison above. That is a reason to price trades separately, not to assume a common benefit add-on for every worker on the residential project. Each source pair belongs to its own classification.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
