What these rates show

Iowa building carpentry runs from a survey line with modest fringe to a union line with a substantial benefit requirement. IA20260036, a decision covering Cedar County, has $14.00 plus $2.04. At IA20260064, a decision covering Dallas County, the union carpenter has $35.50 plus $21.43. The upper base is more than twice the lower one. That spread makes the county decision an early estimating choice, rather than a detail to fill in after the building labor total has already been set.

The electrical comparison also crosses survey and union sources, though the lower electrician fringe is higher than the lower carpenter fringe. IA20260042, a decision covering Dickinson County, prints $22.02 base and $6.36 fringe. The union electrical line in IA20260051, a decision covering Page County, prints $46.40 and $20.89. A survey label does not remove the benefit obligation. Read both components when comparing Iowa building electrical costs, and keep specialty electrical duties outside the general range shown in the main columns.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
