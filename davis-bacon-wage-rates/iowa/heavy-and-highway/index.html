What these rates show

Iowa heavy and highway carpentry has survey rates at both limits, with county and city exceptions on the lines. IA20260003 gives $25.45 plus $15.49 fringe for Clinton, Dubuque, Johnson, Louisa, Muscatne, Des Moines, Cedar and Lee counties, except any of the previously named cities. The higher line in IA20260003 gives $37.53 plus $13.54 for Polk County. The cash rises while the fringe falls. Retain the printed exceptions when costing a heavy and highway crew; a broad county label alone would not describe the lower carpenter rate's full geographic scope.

The cement mason comparison stays within survey rates too. The lower line in IA20260003, a decision covering Adair and 98 other counties, pays $27.40 plus $11.05 fringe, and the higher line in IA20260003, Dubuque County (city of Dubuque), pays $36.98 plus $11.05. Their fringes are the same, so the cash spread carries the difference between these two quotes. The decision covers many counties and contains more detailed work lines. Consult those descriptions rather than treating its first county as the location of either concrete rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
