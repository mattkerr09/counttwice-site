What these rates show

Iowa residential electrical limits both come from union lines, but their cash bases are far apart. IA20260019, a decision covering Scott County, has $19.60 plus $17.81 fringe. IA20260027, a decision covering Mills and 1 other counties, has $46.40 plus $20.89. The benefit amounts are closer than the cash amounts, so the base drives most of this comparison. A residential estimate cannot rely on a single union electrical allowance for all Iowa decisions. Match the contract schedule and complete electrical duties before extending labor hours.

General laborer rates cross a survey-to-union divide instead. The survey line in IA20260005, a decision covering Muscatine County, is $8.411 with $0.00 fringe; the union line in IA20260019, a decision covering Scott County, is $30.06 with $24.10. Benefits add a large cost at the upper end after a cash increase of more than three to one. This difference is separate from the union electrical comparison. The residential crew's mix of trades will change how strongly each pattern affects the bid.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
