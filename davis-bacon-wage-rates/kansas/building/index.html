What these rates show

Kansas building carpentry includes a survey base far below its upper union figure. KS20260007, a decision covering Ellis County, lists $15.94 plus $2.06 fringe. The union line in KS20260052, a decision covering Johnson County, lists $44.63 plus $22.40. The higher cash rate is nearly three times the lower one, with a much larger benefit amount alongside it. Fix the building decision before comparing carpenter hours, because a statewide average allowance would hide this difference between the printed sources.

Plumbing adds another large building spread. The lower survey plumber at KS20260057, a decision covering Sedgwick County, receives $23.12 with $6.86 fringe. The upper union plumber at KS20260052, a decision covering Johnson County, receives $54.28 plus $23.79. The high plumber base exceeds the high carpenter base, and its fringe is also larger. A common rate for skilled building trades would miss those differences even at the same end of the Kansas range. Use each trade's paired figures from its source.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
