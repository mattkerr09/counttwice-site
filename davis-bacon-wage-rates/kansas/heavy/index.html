What these rates show

Kansas heavy carpenter pay spans a low survey line and a union line with far higher benefits. KS20260021, a decision covering Chase and 3 other counties, gives $17.00 plus $1.94 fringe; KS20260014, a decision covering Franklin County, gives $43.28 plus $21.25. The upper cash figure is more than twice the lower one. Where a source covers a county group, preserve the coverage wording rather than naming its first county as if that were the rate's only location. The full line may narrow the coverage further.

The concrete comparison has an even larger proportional base difference. KS20260016, a decision covering Ellis County, lists the survey cement mason at $13.67 with $0.00 fringe. The union line in KS20260004, a decision covering Doniphan County, requires $39.06 and $18.71. The upper line adds a fringe obligation. For heavy work with many concrete hours, the source choice changes both the hourly cash and the benefit cost. Keep those amounts together in the labor estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
