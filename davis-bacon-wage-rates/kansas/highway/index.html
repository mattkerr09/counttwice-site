What these rates show

Kansas road carpentry can move from a zero-fringe survey line to a union line with a large fringe. KS20260137, a decision covering Harvey County, has $13.75 plus $0.00. KS20260140, a decision covering Johnson County, has $43.28 plus $21.25. The upper base is more than three times the lower one. These are highway-covered sources. A carpenter figure copied from another construction type would not help resolve the road contract's county, duties and modification, even if its trade name looked identical.

Cement mason rates on Kansas highways follow a similar survey-to-union pattern, with their own cash and benefit amounts. KS20260086, a decision covering Greenwood County, gives $14.38 base and $0.00 fringe. The union line in KS20260140, a decision covering Johnson County, gives $39.06 plus $18.71. The upper concrete base is lower than the upper carpenter base, and its fringe is lower too. Those trade differences should stay visible when a bid includes both road structures and carpentry hours under the same contract.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
