What these rates show

Kansas residential plumbing has a particularly wide survey-to-union range. KS20260009, a decision covering Miami County, prints $9.701 plus $0.00 fringe. The upper union line in KS20260006, a decision covering Johnson and 1 other counties, prints $54.28 plus $23.79. Its cash base is more than five times the lower base. A residential schedule can therefore carry a substantial union obligation. Find the assigned decision before using a low survey figure as the plumbing allowance for the whole project.

Electrical work also has a zero-fringe survey low and a union high with sizeable benefits. KS20260009, a decision covering Miami County, lists $8.191 with $0.00; KS20260006, a decision covering Johnson and 1 other counties, lists $48.23 with $25.35. The upper electrical base is below the upper plumber base, while its fringe is higher. Taking one common benefit amount for both crews would conceal that difference. For Kansas residential bids, keep the electrical and plumbing calculations separate even where their source coverage is the same.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
