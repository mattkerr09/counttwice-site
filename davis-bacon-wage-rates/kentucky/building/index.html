What these rates show

Kentucky building carpenter rates cross from survey pay with zero fringe to union pay with a large fringe. KY20260099, a decision covering Allen and 3 other counties, gives $18.67 plus $0.00. KY20260101, a decision covering Boyd and 1 other counties, gives $37.04 plus $26.57. The cash base nearly doubles, and benefits widen the total gap further. This is a source and location difference within building work. Decide which contract schedule applies before using carpenter hours to compare a Kentucky building bid with an earlier project.

General laborer costs show a survey-to-union split as well. The line at KY20260099, a decision covering Allen and 3 other counties, has $12.501 base and $0.00 fringe; the line at KY20260092, a decision covering Henderson County, has $28.89 plus $19.20. The upper base is more than twice the lower one. Kentucky's listed equipment examples distinguish a backhoe, excavator or trackhoe line from a crane line. For a building operation involving both digging and lifting, check each machine's complete classification before assigning operator hours.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
