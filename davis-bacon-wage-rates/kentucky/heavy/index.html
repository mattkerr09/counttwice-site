What these rates show

Kentucky heavy general laborer rates carry benefits at both ends of the survey-to-union comparison. KY20260070, a decision covering Hardin County, has $16.18 plus $10.43 fringe. The union line in KY20260067, a decision covering Daviess County, pays $28.94 plus $17.59. The fringe is a sizeable addition even at the lower survey base. Check both columns when setting the heavy crew allowance. A survey source does not mean the contractor can price those hours with cash wages alone.

The electrical base spread is narrower than the laborer base spread, but electrical benefits change sharply. The survey line in KY20260045, a decision covering Ballard County, gives $32.35 with $2.18 fringe. The union line in KY20260062, a decision covering Boone County, gives $40.96 plus $22.44. The benefit increase is larger than the cash increase between these quotes. That matters when comparing a heavy job with a large electrical component against one whose hours are mostly general labor.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
