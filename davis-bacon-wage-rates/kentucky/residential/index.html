What these rates show

Kentucky residential plumbing ranges from a zero-fringe survey line to a union line with substantial benefits. KY20260004, a decision covering Anderson and 11 other counties, has $16.33 plus $0.00; KY20260013, a decision covering Christian and 1 other counties, has $44.26 plus $20.28. The upper cash base is well over twice the lower one. A residential estimator needs the assigned schedule before deciding which wage level to carry. The wide range is a warning about the source difference, not permission to choose the cheaper line for the project.

The concrete spread has a different geographic feature: the upper union line names its county on the line. KY20260002, a decision covering Hart County, prints the survey cement mason at $10.501 with $0.00 fringe. KY20260004 prints $33.60 with $16.05 for Owen County. That local restriction is part of the quoted rate. Keep it with the concrete allowance when reviewing residential bids, rather than assuming the upper amount belongs to every county covered by the wider decision.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
