What these rates show

Louisiana building carpenter rates span survey and union sources, with no fringe at the lower end. LA20260018, a decision covering Concordia, lists $13.081 plus $0.00; LA20260029, a decision covering Acadia, lists $32.59 plus $11.06. The upper cash base is well over twice the lower one. Louisiana decisions use parish coverage, so read the full local scope and duties in the source. The first area listed in a decision is a document reference, not automatically the place assigned to every trade rate.

Electrical pay has a smaller proportional cash spread than carpentry, but benefits are due at both limits. The survey electrician in LA20260036, a decision covering Iberia, receives $20.30 with $5.79 fringe. The union line in LA20260038, a decision covering Jefferson, receives $36.00 plus $16.66. Its benefit amount is larger than the upper carpenter fringe. A building estimate needs the electrical pair on its own, even when a general skilled-trade allowance would be easier to reuse across Louisiana projects.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
