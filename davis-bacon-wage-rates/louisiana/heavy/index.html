What these rates show

Louisiana heavy carpentry compares a broad survey source with a union line that names its own parish group. LA20260002, a decision covering Acadia and 15 other counties, gives $12.811 base and $0.00 fringe. For Ascension, East Baton Rouge, Livingston and West Baton Rouge parishes, LA20260002 gives $32.59 plus $11.06. The higher base is more than twice the lower one. Retain that parish note in the estimate. Broad decision coverage can include places beyond the area assigned to the individual carpenter line.

Concrete work follows a survey-to-union split, with a parish restriction on the upper cement mason line. The survey amount in LA20260002, a decision covering Acadia and 15 other counties, is $13.77 plus $0.00. The union amount for St. James Parish in LA20260005 is $31.83 plus $5.90. The high concrete fringe is much smaller than the high carpenter fringe above. These heavy trades therefore need separate benefit calculations, as well as separate checks that the printed parish coverage fits the work site.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
