What these rates show

Louisiana residential electrical work has a zero-fringe survey low and a much higher union line. LA20260003, a decision covering Allen and 39 other counties, prints $12.501 plus $0.00; LA20260001, Jefferson, Lafourche, Orleans, Plaquemines, St. Bernard, St. Charles, St. James, St. John the Baptist, St. Martin (southern portion), and Terrebonne parishes, prints $36.00 plus $16.66. The upper cash base is close to three times the lower one. Several decisions cover broad parish groups, so a residential bid needs the full coverage and trade notes. A statewide-looking document heading should not become a claim that every electrical line applies to every parish.

The plumbing comparison follows the same source direction with a different pair of wage and benefit amounts. The survey plumber in LA20260003, a decision covering Allen and 39 other counties, has $11.771 with $0.00 fringe. The union plumber in LA20260001, Ascension, East Baton Rouge, Livingston , St. James (northwestern portion), St. Martin (eastern portion), and West Baton Rouge parishes, has $32.42 with $16.50. The high plumbing base is below the high electrical base, while their fringes are fairly close. Cost each crew under its own residential classification, and keep the decision's parish scope beside the calculation used in the bid.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
