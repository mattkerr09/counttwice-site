What these rates show

Maine building carpenter rates include a survey base with modest fringe and a union base roughly twice as high. ME20260008, a decision covering Hancock County, has $15.22 plus $2.46; ME20260004, a decision covering Androscoggin County, has $30.56 plus $19.96. Benefits increase far more sharply than cash between those two lines. That makes the contract's county schedule important to the carpenter total, even before considering specialty duties such as drywall work that have their own descriptions in the table.

Concrete has a larger proportional building spread than carpentry. ME20260006, a decision covering Aroostook County, gives the survey cement mason $16.13 plus $3.82 fringe. The union line at ME20260050, a decision covering Cumberland County, gives $45.38 plus $34.84. The upper fringe is a large addition to a cash base already far above the lower survey line. Price concrete separately from carpentry when assessing a Maine building crew, since a shared percentage uplift would not capture these two trade patterns equally well.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
