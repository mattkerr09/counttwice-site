What these rates show

Maine heavy general laborer rates move from survey pay to union pay with a marked increase in fringe. ME20260021, a decision covering Hancock County, has $12.371 plus $4.37. The union line in ME20260022, a decision covering Kennebec County, has $25.40 plus $21.28. Fringe growth outpaces the increase in cash between Maine's general laborer quotes. Cash roughly doubles, while the benefit amount rises by a larger factor.

Electrical pay is a narrower survey comparison in the heavy set. ME20260020, a decision covering Aroostook County, lists $24.87 base and $8.54 fringe. ME20260031, a decision covering Penobscot County, lists $30.43 with $7.31. The higher electrical base has the smaller fringe of these two lines. That differs from the laborer comparison, where both amounts increase together. A Maine heavy estimate should pair the electrical figures as printed and avoid building an artificial total from the largest numbers in separate columns.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
