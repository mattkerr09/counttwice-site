What these rates show

Maine highway general laborer limits are survey rates with a relatively narrow base range. The lower line in ME20260047, a decision covering Penobscot County, gives $12.831 plus $2.20 fringe. The upper line at ME20260040, a decision covering Oxford County, gives $15.83 plus $1.99. The higher base has a slightly lower fringe. Keep each pair intact when comparing road crew allowances. The small spread here does not justify carrying over a heavy-work union rate or its much larger benefit amount.

The highway electrical spread is narrower still, and both ends are survey sources. ME20260045, a decision covering Androscoggin County, has $25.21 with $5.63 fringe; ME20260046, a decision covering Cumberland County, has $26.40 with $6.66. Benefits increase with cash in this comparison, unlike the general laborer ends above. The county decision still matters even when the base amounts look close. For a long road estimate, a small hourly difference will be repeated over the electrical hours, so use the contract's own source pair.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
