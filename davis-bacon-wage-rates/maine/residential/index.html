What these rates show

Maine's residential electrical comparison is between survey lines in county-group decisions. ME20260003, a decision covering Aroostook and 2 other counties, gives $7.501 plus $0.00 fringe. ME20260002, a decision covering Androscoggin and 2 other counties, gives $13.78 plus $2.26. The higher base is substantially above the lower one, and only the upper line carries a dollar fringe. These are decision coverage groups rather than inferred locations for individual lines. Review the complete electrical scope before using one group decision for a residential site.

Plumbing has a smaller residential cash difference than electrical work. The survey plumber in ME20260002, a decision covering Androscoggin and 2 other counties, receives $7.832 plus $0.00; the upper survey line in ME20260005, a decision covering Franklin and 9 other counties, receives $9.503 plus $0.72. Its upper fringe is modest, but it still belongs in the calculation. The lower plumbing and lower electrical figures come from different county-group sources, so the cheaper group for one trade should not be assumed to be the cheaper group for every residential crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
