What these rates show

Maryland building carpenter limits both come from state-adopted lines. MD20260081, a decision covering Allegany County, lists $30.71 plus $22.54 fringe. MD20260112, a decision covering Cecil County, lists $37.08 plus $25.48. The cash spread is moderate, but benefits are substantial at both ends. State-adopted is the source kind printed in this source set; it does not mean a single statewide carpenter amount. Keep the county decision with the building estimate so the applicable local obligation remains clear.

The electrical base difference is much wider than the carpenter difference. The state-adopted electrician at MD20260123, a decision covering Somerset County, is $32.55 with $16.58 fringe. MD20260120, a decision covering Montgomery County, carries $59.50 with $23.06. Both the wage and benefit amounts rise at the upper end. A Maryland building allowance based on carpentry alone would not describe the electrical spread. Use the electrical line's own scope and paired fringe when comparing the cost of crews in different county decisions.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
