What these rates show

Maryland heavy and highway electrical pay varies substantially across the state-adopted schedules. MD20260029, a decision covering Anne Arundel County, prints $36.84 base plus $19.62 fringe. The upper line at MD20260038, a decision covering Frederick County, prints $59.50 plus $23.06. The fringe difference is smaller than the base difference, but the benefit amount is sizeable in both sources. A heavy and highway bid needs the assigned county decision, rather than a general state-adopted allowance carried from another Maryland project. For the electrical portion of a heavy and highway estimate, write down the chosen source beside the planned installation. The low and high columns answer a comparison question across the schedules. They do not assign a rate to an unnamed site. If the decision lacks a trade needed for the installation, follow the conformance process below instead of choosing whichever familiar trade has the lowest cash amount. Keep the base and its companion fringe together while checking that classification. A benefits allowance borrowed from another county could change the total even where the two cash figures look close. Anyone taking over the estimate should be able to trace both components to the same printed work line, then confirm the modification incorporated in the project contract. That source review belongs before the labor quantity is multiplied by an hourly allowance.

General laborer bases vary even more in proportional terms. The state-adopted line in MD20260032, a decision covering Calvert County, is $16.12 with $0.34 fringe. The upper line in MD20260041, a decision covering Howard County, is $34.32 with $9.10. The cash more than doubles, and the benefit amount also grows sharply. Maryland's heavy and highway equipment examples distinguish excavators, cranes and tower cranes as separate work descriptions. Read the named machine before using an operator allowance. Similar lifting or digging work does not make two equipment lines interchangeable. The truck details also separate flatbed, dump and articulating dump work. Keep those duties apart when costing a heavy and highway crew with several kinds of equipment. The operator range is a comparison across included machine lines, not a single rate that can be assigned to every machine on the project.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
