What these rates show

Maryland residential electrical pay includes both a zero-fringe survey line and a union line with substantial fringe. MD20260069, a decision covering Washington County, prints $18.00 plus $0.00. At MD20260059, a decision covering Allegany County, the union electrician receives $44.00 plus $20.69. The higher base is more than twice the lower one. This source mix differs from the state-adopted building comparisons. A residential estimate needs its own decision, even if the crew has recently worked on another Maryland construction type.

Residential carpentry has survey lines at both limits rather than the electrical survey-to-union split. MD20260001, a decision covering Dorchester County, gives $15.79 with $1.20 fringe. MD20260066, a decision covering Prince George's County, gives $26.38 plus $7.00. Both pay components rise at the upper end, though the cash spread is smaller than for electricians. Keep that distinction in a mixed residential crew estimate. The source kind and benefit amount for one Maryland trade should not be assumed to describe the others.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
