What these rates show

Massachusetts building carpenter limits are union lines within one broad decision. MA20260001, Barnstable; Bristol (except Attleboro & North Attleboro); and Norfolk (Avon, Holbrook, Randolph, Stoughton) counties, lists a lower base of $39.28 plus $27.90 fringe. Its upper line gives $61.19 plus $31.55 in MA20260001, a decision covering Barnstable and 7 other counties. Cash accounts for most of this spread. Because the decision number stays the same, the full classification and local notes inside the document must do the work of distinguishing rates. A broad coverage heading cannot select a carpenter line by itself.

The electrician comparison is also all union within the building decision. MA20260001, a decision covering Barnstable and 7 other counties, gives $48.16 base and $36.20 fringe; MA20260001, a decision covering Barnstable and 7 other counties, gives $66.17 with $37.08. These two fringes are close, despite a significant cash difference. Read the electrical work and area notes before copying the upper amount into a bid. The decision's first listed county is not evidence that either electrician rate is confined to that county or applies everywhere else in the source.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
