What these rates show

Massachusetts heavy electrical limits come from union lines in different decision sources. MA20260002, a decision covering Worcester County, carries $48.16 plus $36.20 fringe. MA20260008, a decision covering Barnstable and 8 other counties, carries $66.17 plus $37.08. The cash range is appreciable while the two fringe amounts are close. This is a local schedule comparison within heavy work, rather than a lower survey rate against a higher union rate. Keep the source and complete electrical duties with the heavy crew calculation.

Carpentry has an union heavy comparison too, with a smaller cash range than electrical work. MA20260010, Franklin County (Erving, Orange, North Orange, and Warwick), has $43.33 with $27.82 fringe. The upper carpenter line in MA20260008, Middlesex (Belmont, Cambridge, Everett, Malden, Medford, Somerville); Norfolk (Brookline, Dedham, Milton); and Suffolk counties, has $61.19 with $31.55. Both amounts increase at the upper end. Some sources cover county groups, so these quotations use decision coverage wording when the line has no separate area note. Read the original local restrictions before treating a broad decision as one uniform carpenter rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
