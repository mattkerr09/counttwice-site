What these rates show

Massachusetts highway carpenter bases can roughly double within survey rates. MA20260019, a decision covering Hampden County, gives $33.03 plus $20.02 fringe. MA20260024, a decision covering Suffolk County, gives $66.59 with $15.41. The higher cash line has a lower fringe, so the base difference is not the whole difference in labor cost. A highway bid needs the actual pair from the contract line, rather than a union assumption or a common statewide carpenter allowance.

Electrical sources follow a different pattern from the survey carpenter comparison. The survey electrician in MA20260023, a decision covering Plymouth County, receives $45.13 plus $13.86. The union line at MA20260017, a decision covering Bristol County, receives $66.17 plus $37.08. Here both base and benefits rise sharply. A contractor estimating several Massachusetts highway trades should therefore check each source kind separately. The highest cash amount and the largest fringe in the table can belong to different lines, so keep the chosen electrical pair together.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
