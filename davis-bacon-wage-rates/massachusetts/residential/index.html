What these rates show

Massachusetts residential carpenter pay has more than one union base inside a broad decision. The lower line in MA20260004, marked a decision covering Barnstable and 10 other counties, is $39.28 plus $27.90 fringe. The upper line in MA20260004, a decision covering Barnstable and 10 other counties, is $61.19 plus $31.55. That is a substantial difference in both components. The lower line names its own county group and town limits. Read that full note before assigning the carpenter amount to a residential site within the decision's wider coverage.

Electrician limits are union figures too, with fringes that remain close while base wages change. MA20260004, a decision covering Barnstable and 10 other counties, prints $48.16 plus $36.20; MA20260004, a decision covering Barnstable and 10 other counties, prints $66.17 plus $37.08. The cash spread is much smaller proportionally than for carpenters. A residential bid with both trades should not apply one percentage change across the crew. Resolve the electrical work description and local note inside the decision before deciding which source line supplies the labor allowance.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
