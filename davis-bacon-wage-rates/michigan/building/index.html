What these rates show

Michigan building carpentry spans a survey line and a union line with a narrower local restriction. MI20260120, a decision covering Gogebic County, gives $13.001 plus $1.32 fringe. The upper union line in MI20260090, marked remainder of county, has $42.44 plus $31.43. The higher cash base is more than three times the lower one. A phrase such as remainder of county must be read with its surrounding source text; it cannot safely be reduced to the first county in the decision.

The electrical building range is also wide. The survey electrician at MI20260152, a decision covering St Joseph County, is $17.00 with $3.51 fringe; the union electrician at MI20260084, a decision covering Ingham County, is $56.05 with $30.52. The upper fringe adds a large cost after an already substantial cash increase. These are separate electrical obligations, not a statewide rate. Match the project location and complete electrical classification before using the amounts to assess a building crew's weekly payroll or labor estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
