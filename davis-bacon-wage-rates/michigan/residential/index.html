What these rates show

Michigan residential general laborer rates include survey pay and a union line with a much higher base. MI20260018, a decision covering Muskegon County, lists $13.031 plus $4.65 fringe. MI20260017, a decision covering Monroe County, lists $38.58 with $15.70. The upper cash amount is close to three times the lower amount. That gap matters when a residential bid contains many general labor hours.

The residential carpenter range has a county-group restriction on its upper union line. The survey carpenter in MI20260003, a decision covering Grand Traverse County, has $13.002 plus $3.39. The union carpenter for Arenac, Clare, Gladwin, Gratiot, Huron, Isabella, Midland & Shiawassee counties in MI20260008 has $36.46 plus $23.69. Keep the whole printed group with the rate. A broad Michigan decision can cover additional places, and the first name in its coverage list cannot replace the local restriction attached to this carpenter line. The Michigan residential table names pickup-truck work, including a line for Macomb and Wayne counties. Its equipment examples group backhoes, excavators, loaders and rollers. Those work and county details belong with the crew estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
