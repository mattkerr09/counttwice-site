What these rates show

Minnesota building carpenter limits are state-adopted lines with a benefit pattern that deserves attention. MN20260113, a decision covering Yellow Medicine County, has $33.00 plus $4.36 fringe. MN20260043, a decision covering Cottonwood County, has $60.38 with $0.00. The higher cash line has zero fringe, while the lower cash line has a separate benefit amount. Compare the paired obligations rather than assuming the larger base must also carry a larger fringe or treating state-adopted as one statewide price.

Electrical pay has an even wider cash range within the state-adopted building sources. MN20260063, a decision covering Lac Qui Parle County, prints $20.00 with $0.00 fringe; MN20260103, a decision covering Todd County, prints $67.50 with $10.73. Here the upper line also adds a benefit obligation, unlike the high carpenter line above. Those trade-specific patterns matter when comparing Minnesota building crew totals. Use the electrical line in the assigned county decision, and preserve its own fringe instead of carrying over a carpenter assumption.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
