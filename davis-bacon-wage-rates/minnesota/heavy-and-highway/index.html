What these rates show

Minnesota heavy and highway plumbing has state-adopted rates with a large base and benefit spread. MN20260232, a decision covering Beltrami and 10 other counties, lists $30.00 plus $0.00 fringe. The upper line at MN20260235, a decision covering Benton and 4 other counties, lists $61.28 plus $31.52. The cash base more than doubles, and the upper line adds substantial fringe where the lower line has none. These sources cover county groups. Read any narrower work or area notes inside them before using this comparison to price plumbing on a heavy and highway project. The equipment examples show why capacity and attachments deserve attention before a machine allowance is entered. One printed group describes hydraulic backhoes with shovel-type controls and a rated-capacity limit that includes attachments. Two other groups separate bituminous rollers under eight tons from those eight tons and over. These are work limits, so identify the planned machine before choosing a rate. The paving group also names both the operator and screed person. Keep the full description with the takeoff when those duties are planned. A broad operator range cannot decide which equipment group applies. Stationary concrete mixing equipment appears alongside mobile digging equipment in another long list. Read through the linked determination for the remaining duties and qualifications after each shortened quotation. Copy any local coverage limits with the chosen group so the estimate can be checked against the actual site.

Electrical work also uses state-adopted sources at both ends, but its lower line already carries sizeable fringe. MN20260237, a decision covering Blue Earth and 5 other counties, has $43.25 base and $12.86 fringe. MN20260235, a decision covering Benton and 4 other counties, has $63.59 with $35.79. The upper benefit amount is a large part of the electrical total. A shared source kind does not make the local obligations alike. Keep the electrician's paired amounts and the relevant county-group decision with the estimate for the Minnesota heavy and highway crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
