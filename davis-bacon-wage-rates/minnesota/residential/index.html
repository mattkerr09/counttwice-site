What these rates show

Minnesota residential plumbing has a very wide survey-to-union spread. MN20260018, a decision covering Swift County, gives the survey plumber $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. MN20260008, a decision covering McLeod and 1 other counties, gives the union plumber $59.54 plus $33.26. The upper cash base is more than eight times the lower one. That is a source difference to resolve before costing the crew, not a range from which to pick an allowance. A residential contract can carry the much higher union obligation shown here.

The electrical range is similarly wide, with zero fringe at the survey end and substantial fringe at the union end. MN20260004, a decision covering Becker and 3 other counties, has $7.252 with $0.00; MN20260019, a decision covering Hennepin County, has $59.00 with $35.79. The lower source covers several counties, so its first county is not presented as the electrician rate's own location. Review the full residential decision for duties and local scope before using either figure in a Minnesota housing estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
