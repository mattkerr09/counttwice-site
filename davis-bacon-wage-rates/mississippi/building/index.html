What these rates show

Mississippi building carpenter rates move from a survey line with modest fringe to a union line with a much larger fringe. MS20260042, a decision covering Smith County, lists $15.00 plus $1.88. The upper union line at MS20260052, a decision covering Jackson County, lists $31.36 plus $13.45. The higher base is more than twice the lower one. Determine the building source before extending carpenter hours, since both pay components can change substantially when an allowance is copied from another Mississippi project.

Electrical benefits are sizeable even at the lower survey base. MS20260046, a decision covering Attala and 14 other counties, gives $17.03 with $9.10 fringe. The union electrical line in MS20260042, a decision covering Smith County, gives $33.30 plus $11.63. The benefit spread is much smaller than the cash spread in this comparison. That differs from the carpenter pattern above. A building estimator should read each trade's paired source amounts instead of assuming a low survey wage means the whole benefit obligation is small.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
