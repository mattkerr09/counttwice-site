What these rates show

Mississippi heavy electrical limits compare survey and union lines with benefits at both ends. The survey line in MS20260057, a decision covering Lafayette County, is $19.36 plus $7.70 fringe. The union line in MS20260058, a decision covering Scott County, is $33.30 plus $11.63. The cash increase is the larger part of this spread, though the fringe also rises. These heavy sources belong with the electrical estimate; a rate from a residential or building source would need a separate schedule decision.

General laborer limits are survey figures, with a different benefit pattern from electricians. MS20260068, a decision covering Lauderdale County, gives $9.001 base and $1.88 fringe. The higher cash line in MS20260061, a decision covering Jackson County, gives $15.05 with $0.00. That upper line has zero fringe even though the lower cash line does not. Compare the total pair in the original heavy source. Combining the larger base with the other line's fringe would invent a laborer obligation that is not printed on either line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
