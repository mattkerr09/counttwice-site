What these rates show

Mississippi highway electrical rates include a survey low and a union high. MS20260079, a decision covering Alcorn County, prints $21.80 plus $7.93 fringe; MS20260087, a decision covering Clarke County, prints $33.30 plus $11.63. Benefits are a meaningful addition at both ends. A road bid needs the county-covered decision and the complete electrical duties, rather than a statewide electrician allowance. The table leaves other electrical specialties under their own named work so a restricted line does not lower the general comparison.

Concrete offers a narrower survey highway comparison with zero fringe at both ends. The cement mason in MS20260092, a decision covering Jefferson County, is $11.291 plus $0.00. The line at MS20260090, a decision covering Holmes County, is $15.69 plus $0.00. Cash accounts for the whole difference between these two quotes. Keep the concrete and electrical items separate in the road estimate. Mississippi highway cement masons have their own source mix and fringe pattern.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
