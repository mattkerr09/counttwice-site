What these rates show

Mississippi residential electrical pay has a survey low and a union high whose county note must travel with the rate. MS20260004, a decision covering Alcorn County, gives $9.381 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line in MS20260022 gives $32.90 plus $11.10 for Jefferson Davis and Marion counties. The upper base is more than three times the lower one. Check that printed county group before using the union amount, since the broader decision coverage can include places outside an individual electrical line's scope.

Carpentry has a much smaller residential cash spread and survey lines at both ends. MS20260012, a decision covering Jones County, lists $9.352 with $0.00 fringe; MS20260006, a decision covering Bolivar County, lists $14.00 with $0.00. Both benefit amounts are zero. The electrical union-to-survey gap therefore should not be used as a general uplift for every Mississippi residential trade. A carpenter allowance needs its own local source, even when the benefit calculation is simpler than the one for the electrical crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
