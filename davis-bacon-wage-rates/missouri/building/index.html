What these rates show

Missouri building carpentry spans a survey line with benefits and a union line with higher cash and fringe. MO20260013, a decision covering Lawrence County, lists $18.22 plus $7.85. MO20260072, a decision covering Cass County, lists $44.63 plus $22.40. The upper cash base is more than twice the lower one. The lower survey fringe is still a real part of the obligation. Start the building estimate with the assigned decision so that neither a survey nor a union label becomes shorthand for an assumed total cost.

Plumbing has a larger cash spread than carpentry in these building sources. The survey plumber at MO20260013, a decision covering Lawrence County, receives $22.25 with $9.77 fringe. The upper union line at MO20260007, a decision covering Carroll County, receives $58.38 plus $25.29. Both bases have benefits beside them, and both components rise in the upper quote. Read the plumbing classification on its own before pricing the crew. Its wage and fringe pair is different from the carpenter pair even where the lower decision is shared.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
