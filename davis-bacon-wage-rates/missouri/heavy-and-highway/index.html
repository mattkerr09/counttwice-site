What these rates show

Missouri heavy and highway electrical limits are union lines in one decision, separated by their own county groups. MO20260001 gives $25.40 plus $12.19 fringe for Barry, Barton, Cedar, Dade, Jasper, Lawrence, McDonald, Newton, St Clair, and Vernon counties. The upper line at MO20260001 gives $54.01 plus $27.85 for Bates, Benton, Carroll, Cass, Clay, Cooper, Henry, Jackson, Johnson, Lafayette, Morgan, Pettis, Platte, Ray and Saline counties:. The cash more than doubles inside the same document. A decision number alone is therefore not enough for the electrical estimate. Keep the complete line-level county list with the base and benefits copied into the bid. Equipment work illustrates another limit on using the statewide range. The selected descriptions include asphalt finishing machines, cranes and concrete mixers in long equipment groups. Other groups name chip spreaders, rollers and back fillers. Find the intended machine in the full group before copying an allowance. Read every duty in the equipment list before using a familiar machine name to choose a rate. Keep the work description and any local restriction from the source with the bid so the planned equipment can be checked when the crew is assigned.

The heavy and highway plumber comparison also uses union lines with distinct county notes. For Barton, Jasper, McDonald and Newton counties, MO20260001 lists $32.78 plus $15.32 fringe. For Cass, Clay, Jackson, Johnson, and Platte counties, MO20260001 lists $56.63 plus $24.54. These groups are different from the electrical groups above. Do not assume every trade in the same Missouri heavy and highway determination follows the same local divisions. Match the plumber's own coverage and full duties, then carry both amounts from that line into the labor calculation.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
