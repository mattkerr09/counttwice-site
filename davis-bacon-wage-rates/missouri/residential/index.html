What these rates show

Missouri residential electrical work includes a zero-fringe survey line and a union line with much higher cash and benefits. MO20260058, a decision covering Adair and 29 other counties, gives $15.53 plus $0.00. For Bates, Caldwell, Cass, Clay, Clinton, Lafayette, Platte & Ray counties, MO20260066 gives $47.37 plus $25.89. The upper base is more than three times the lower base. Its county group comes from the electrical line itself. That restriction needs to stay in the bid notes rather than being replaced with the first county in the decision.

General laborer rates show another survey-to-union spread, but their locations come from the decision coverage where no narrower note is printed. MO20260063, a decision covering Jackson County, has $11.001 base and $0.00 fringe. MO20260070, a decision covering Crawford and 6 other counties, has $34.07 plus $14.85. The upper cash more than triples the lower cash and adds a benefit obligation. Missouri's residential equipment examples distinguish roller work from backhoe and excavator work. A housing site that needs both compaction and digging should carry each machine's description into the estimate, alongside the separate electrical and general laborer hours.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
