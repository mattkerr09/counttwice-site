What these rates show

Montana building carpentry compares a survey line with a union line marked by zone. MT20260028, a decision covering Daniels County, gives $16.70 plus $2.31 fringe. The union carpenter in MT20260033, under Zone 1, gives $32.05 plus $15.02. The base nearly doubles while benefits grow much more sharply. The zone needs its definition from the decision, so a county name taken from the coverage list cannot by itself establish where the upper carpenter rate applies.

The plumber range has benefits due at both the lower survey base and upper union base. MT20260046, a decision covering Petroleum County, prints $25.75 with $10.87 fringe; MT20260025, a decision covering Beaverhead County, prints $46.45 plus $19.92. These benefit amounts are substantial relative to the cash wages. Unlike the upper carpenter quote, the plumber quote here is located through decision coverage wording. Read the full plumbing line for any additional work or local conditions before calculating Montana building crew costs.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
