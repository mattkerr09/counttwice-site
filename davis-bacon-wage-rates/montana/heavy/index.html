What these rates show

Montana heavy carpenter rates include a survey low and a zone-marked union high. MT20260069, a decision covering Custer County, has $21.13 plus $7.00 fringe. MT20260065 has $36.49 plus $14.33 under Zone 1. The higher cash and benefit amounts both change the heavy crew total. Keep the zone definition with that union rate when checking a project location. The decision's broad county coverage is useful for finding the document, but it does not replace the zone printed beside the carpenter line.

Electrical pay has an union heavy comparison, so the carpenter survey-to-union explanation does not fit this trade. The line at MT20260075, a decision covering Beaverhead and 11 other counties, is $29.59 with $14.13 fringe. The upper line at MT20260065, a decision covering Carbon County, is $43.92 plus $18.17. Most of the difference between these two quotes is cash. Match the electrical duties and local reach in the contract decision before carrying a rate into another Montana heavy estimate, even if both sources are union schedules.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
