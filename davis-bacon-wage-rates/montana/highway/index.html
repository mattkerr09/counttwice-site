What these rates show

Montana highway carpentry has one included general base in the state-adopted schedule. MT20260080, a decision covering Beaverhead and 57 other counties, lists $43.78 plus $17.07 fringe. There is no lower survey carpenter line in this highway range to compare with that amount. A flat range makes the general cash figure easy to see, but it does not price specialized carpenter work. Review the duties and any local notes in the highway source before extending the crew hours for the road contract.

The included electrical range is flat as well. The state-adopted electrician in MT20260080, a decision covering Beaverhead and 57 other counties, has $48.31 base and $22.00 fringe. Its base and benefit amount are both higher than the carpenter pair quoted above. Those trade differences remain relevant even when neither trade shows a spread across its general lines. Keep electrical hours under their own classification, and use the full Montana highway decision for equipment groups and other work outside the common trade rows summarized here.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
