What these rates show

Montana residential electrician limits are survey rates, with the upper cash base more than twice the lower one. MT20260019, a decision covering Carter and 11 other counties, gives $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. MT20260018, a decision covering Cascade County, gives $15.90 plus $2.01. Only the upper line has a dollar fringe in this pair. A residential bid still needs the county-covered source even without a union line at either end. The survey label alone does not identify the electrical cost for a Montana project.

Plumbing shows a similar residential cash ratio but uses its own local sources. The survey plumber at MT20260019, a decision covering Carter and 11 other counties, receives $7.701 with $0.00 fringe; the one at MT20260009, a decision covering Silver Bow County, receives $16.45 with $1.95. The benefit amount is modest at the upper end, yet it must remain with the selected base. The upper electrical and plumbing decisions are different. A county source chosen for one residential trade should not be assumed to set the upper or lower cost for the other.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
