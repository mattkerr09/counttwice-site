What these rates show

Nebraska heavy electrical limits are union rates, with a zone marker on the lower line. NE20260034 gives $35.00 plus $18.88 fringe under Zone 1. The upper line at NE20260038, a decision covering Dodge County, gives $46.40 plus $20.89. The fringe amounts are much closer than the cash amounts. The zone definition belongs with the lower quote. Do not assign it to the decision's first county as though the coverage list supplied the electrician line's actual boundary.

General laborer rates follow an survey pattern instead of the union electrical pattern. NE20260038, a decision covering Dodge County, has $11.691 base and $1.31 fringe. NE20260051, a decision covering Sarpy County, has $17.31 plus $4.03. Both parts rise at the upper end, but the dollar obligations are much smaller than the electrical ones. Keep the trade calculations separate in a Nebraska heavy bid. Nebraska's heavy equipment descriptions name backhoes and excavators, with trackhoes included in another work description. Dump-truck work appears under truck drivers. A bid with excavation and hauling needs the machine and vehicle duties checked alongside the laborer hours.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
