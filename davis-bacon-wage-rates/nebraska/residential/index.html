What these rates show

Nebraska residential plumbing spans a very low survey line and a union line with sizeable fringe. NE20260015, a decision covering Cedar and 4 other counties, prints $7.251 plus $0.00. NE20260003, a decision covering Cass and 2 other counties, prints $44.26 plus $16.90. The upper base is more than six times the lower base. A residential designation alone therefore does not indicate a low wage obligation. Establish the contract's county-group decision and plumbing duties before accepting a labor allowance based on a different Nebraska residential schedule.

Carpentry also has a zero-fringe survey low and a union high. The line at NE20260014, a decision covering Clay and 3 other counties, is $7.252 with $0.00 fringe; the line at NE20260003, a decision covering Cass and 2 other counties, is $33.42 with $18.66. The high carpenter base is below the high plumber base, but its fringe is larger. Keep those paired obligations separate for the residential crew. A benefit amount carried from plumbing into carpentry would not reproduce what the Nebraska source lines print.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
