What these rates show

Nevada building carpentry includes state-adopted and union lines. The lower state-adopted quote at NV20260077, a decision covering Churchill County, is $43.13 plus $25.29 fringe. The union line in NV20260001, marked Zone 1, is $55.85 plus $28.28. Benefits are sizeable at both ends. The upper zone needs the definitions in the full decision. A county from that document's opening coverage list cannot substitute for the zone rule when locating a building carpenter rate.

The electrical comparison has a different benefit pattern within state-adopted sources. NV20260080, a decision covering Lincoln County, lists $48.12 base with $21.56 fringe. NV20260093, a decision covering Washoe County, lists the higher base of $66.94 with $0.00. The high cash line has zero fringe, so the highest base is not automatically the highest paired cash-and-fringe obligation. For a Nevada building bid, compare the complete electrical pairs before drawing conclusions from the base range alone.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
