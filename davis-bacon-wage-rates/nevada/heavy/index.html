What these rates show

Nevada heavy electrician limits are state-adopted lines whose fringes move in the opposite direction from their bases. NV20260065, a decision covering Lincoln County, gives $48.12 plus $21.56 fringe; NV20260073, a decision covering Washoe County, gives $66.94 with $0.00. The upper cash line has no dollar fringe. That makes it especially important to cost the pair printed on each line. Adding the largest table fringe to the largest base would create an electrical total that neither of these heavy sources states.

Carpentry has a state-adopted low and a union high marked by zone. NV20260060, a decision covering Churchill County, prints $43.13 with $25.29 fringe. NV20260001 prints $55.85 plus $28.28 under Zone 1. Both components rise for carpenters, unlike the electrical comparison above. Read the zone definition before using the union carpenter amount on a heavy project. The different source kinds and local restrictions mean one general Nevada wage uplift cannot accurately describe both trades.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
