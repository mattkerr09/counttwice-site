What these rates show

New Hampshire building carpenter limits both come from survey lines, with benefits due at each end. NH20260020, a decision covering Cheshire and 1 other counties, lists $21.46 plus $4.22 fringe; NH20260019, a decision covering Merrimack County, lists $26.19 plus $9.06. The cash spread is modest compared with the change in benefits. A building carpenter allowance needs both amounts from its county source. Survey rates can still carry meaningful fringe costs, so a source label should not stand in for reading the benefit column.

Electrical work has a wider building spread and introduces a union line at the upper end. The survey electrician at NH20260018, a decision covering Grafton County, receives $25.29 with $3.91 fringe. The union line at NH20260015, a decision covering Belknap County, receives $36.12 plus $22.92. The benefit increase is larger than the cash increase. New Hampshire's building carpenter and electrical comparisons therefore have different cost patterns. Keep the trades separate when checking whether the building bid covers the full wage and fringe obligation.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
