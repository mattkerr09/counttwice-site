What these rates show

New Hampshire heavy electrical pay has a substantial survey-to-union spread. NH20260026, a decision covering Strafford County, gives $25.70 plus $11.47 fringe. The union line at NH20260004, a decision covering Belknap County, gives $47.58 plus $29.90. The upper base approaches twice the lower one, while the fringe more than doubles. Identify the heavy determination before extending electrical hours, because both components can change materially between county sources even when the trade name stays the same.

General laborer benefits change especially sharply between the heavy limits. NH20260024, a decision covering Hillsborough, has a survey base of $16.93 with $1.70 fringe. The union line in NH20260009, a decision covering Merrimack County, has $26.34 plus $21.43. The benefit increase exceeds the base increase between these quotes. A cash-only comparison would miss most of the change in the paired obligation.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
