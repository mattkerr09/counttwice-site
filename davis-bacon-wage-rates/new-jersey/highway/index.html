What these rates show

New Jersey highway electrical work includes union lines with detailed local boundaries. NJ20260001 lists $37.35 plus $27.17 fringe for a decision covering Atlantic and 9 other counties. The upper line in NJ20260001, a decision covering Atlantic and 9 other counties, lists $63.82 plus $41.99. Roads form part of the lower line's stated restriction, so a county-wide label would be too broad. Keep the route directions with the electrical allowance and review them when the highway contract extends through different parts of the covered area.

Cement mason limits are union lines too, with fringes that move differently from base wages. NJ20260002, a decision covering Bergen and 10 other counties, gives $43.56 with $32.50 fringe. The higher base in NJ20260002, a decision covering Bergen and 10 other counties, is $54.64 with $30.95. Its fringe is slightly lower. That matters when comparing paired concrete costs, and it shows why the table's largest base and largest fringe should not be added automatically. Read the exact concrete line and any area note inside the New Jersey highway decision.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
