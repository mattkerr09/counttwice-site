What these rates show

New Jersey residential electrical work uses both survey and union sources. The survey electrician in NJ20260014 has $37.89 plus $16.10 fringe from a decision covering Mercer County. The union line at NJ20260007, Burlington County - north of a line following the west and south limits of Burlington Borough from the Delaware River, in a southeasterly direction, to the Burlington-Mt. The upper line follows roads and a rail line through Burlington County. Keep those directions with the estimate when assigning electrical hours. Use the source's own construction type and local work description when planning a residential crew.

Residential plumbing has a smaller base ratio than electrical work, but the union benefit difference is large. NJ20260005, a decision covering Atlantic County, prints $31.50 base with $13.41 fringe. NJ20260014, a decision covering Mercer County, prints $62.09 with $45.05. The higher cash amount is nearly twice the lower one, and its fringe is much more than twice as large. Keep the source pairs together when comparing residential plumbing bids. The upper electrical or carpenter benefit amount should not replace the fringe on the chosen plumber line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
