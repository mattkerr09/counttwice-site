What these rates show

New Mexico heavy electrician limits both come from union lines. NM20260038, a decision covering Bernalillo County, gives $34.50 plus $10.81 fringe. The upper line in NM20260011 gives $46.35 plus $13.65 for Los Alamos County. The high cash figure carries a county note on the line itself. Keep that note in the bid file rather than substituting a place from the wider decision coverage. The source kind stays union while the local wage and benefit amounts change.

Carpentry has an survey heavy comparison, with zero fringe at the lower base and benefits at the upper base. NM20260038, a decision covering Bernalillo County, prints $16.43 plus $0.00. NM20260007, a decision covering Curry County, prints $22.26 plus $6.20. The base spread is smaller than for electricians, and the source mix is different. Use the carpenter's own line when calculating heavy crew costs. An electrical union allowance or its benefit amount would not describe these New Mexico survey carpenter obligations.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
