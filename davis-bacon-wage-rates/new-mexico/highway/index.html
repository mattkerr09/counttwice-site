What these rates show

New Mexico highway electrical pay has survey and union lines with a fairly modest cash difference. NM20260035, a decision covering Dona Ana and 1 other counties, lists $24.46 plus $8.56 fringe. The union line in NM20260034, a decision covering Santa Fe County, lists $29.79 plus $12.74. Benefits are due at both ends and increase with the base. The road contract's decision still matters even though this spread is much narrower than some building or heavy comparisons. Keep the construction schedule and electrical duties with the amount copied into the estimate.

The highway general laborer comparison remains within survey rates. NM20260037, a decision covering Cibola and 11 other counties, has $9.601 base and $0.35 fringe; NM20260036, a decision covering Catron and 13 other counties, has $12.732 plus $0.35. The fringes are equal, so the cash difference carries the change between these two quotes. Both documents cover county groups, which are source coverage rather than an inferred location for every laborer line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
