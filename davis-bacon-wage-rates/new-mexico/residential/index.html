What these rates show

New Mexico residential plumbing includes a survey line with zero fringe and a union line with a larger base and benefits. NM20260002, a decision covering McKinley and 1 other counties, gives $11.071 plus $0.00. NM20260001, a decision covering Bernalillo County, gives $22.84 plus $7.33. The cash base more than doubles at the upper end. Resolve the residential county source before multiplying plumbing hours, since the trade name and residential label alone do not tell an estimator which of these very different obligations belongs to the contract.

Concrete has a smaller survey residential spread. The cement mason at NM20260002, a decision covering McKinley and 1 other counties, is $8.961 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line at NM20260005, a decision covering Catron and 13 other counties, is $12.502 with $0.00. Both fringes are zero, unlike the upper plumbing line above. Keep that distinction in a residential crew estimate. These concrete decisions cover groups of counties, so their first listed county should not become a claim about the individual line's local reach without reading the full source.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
