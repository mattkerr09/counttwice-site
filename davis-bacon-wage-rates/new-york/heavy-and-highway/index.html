What these rates show

New York heavy and highway concrete work has a very wide survey-to-union spread. The survey cement mason at NY20260032, a decision covering Ontario County, receives $15.75 plus $0.59 fringe. The union line at NY20260003, a decision covering Bronx and 4 other counties, receives $59.87 plus $41.81. The upper cash base is nearly four times the lower one, with a large benefit obligation added. Decide which heavy and highway source covers the project before extending concrete hours. A low survey allowance from another county decision can miss both parts of the upper obligation.

The plumber comparison stays within union rates and has fringes that are close despite a wide cash spread. NY20260002, a decision covering Albany and 9 other counties, gives $31.40 base with $31.70 fringe. NY20260003, a decision covering Bronx and 4 other counties, gives $73.00 plus $32.81. The lower fringe slightly exceeds its cash base. Broad county-group decisions supply both quotes, so these are source coverage descriptions rather than claims that the first county owns the rate. How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
