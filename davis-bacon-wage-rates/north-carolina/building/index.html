What these rates show

North Carolina building carpenter limits are survey rates with benefits at both ends. NC20260056, a decision covering Pasquotank County, gives $14.64 plus $2.39 fringe. NC20260008, a decision covering Mecklenburg County, gives $23.73 plus $5.42. Both amounts increase in the upper quote, without a union source entering the comparison. A survey label does not create a statewide carpenter price. Match the county-covered decision and full carpenter duties before carrying an allowance from one North Carolina building bid to another.

Electrical work follows a survey-to-union pattern instead of the survey carpenter pattern. The survey electrician at NC20260055, a decision covering Northampton County, is $15.09 with $0.92 fringe. The union line in NC20260005, a decision covering Gaston County, is $32.31 with $13.66. The upper cash rate is more than twice the lower rate, and its fringe is much larger. Price electrical hours separately from carpentry so the building estimate reflects this different source mix and benefit obligation.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
