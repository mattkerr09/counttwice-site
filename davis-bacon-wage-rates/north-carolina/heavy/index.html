What these rates show

North Carolina heavy general laborer limits are survey rates with a relatively small cash spread. NC20260078, a decision covering Brunswick and 2 other counties, lists $9.211 plus $0.00 fringe. NC20260076, a decision covering Alexander and 3 other counties, lists $11.302 plus $1.53. Only the upper pair carries a dollar fringe here. The source documents cover county groups, so the opening county is not used as the rate's own place.

The included general carpenter range has just one base in this heavy reading. The survey line in NC20260072, a decision covering Alamance County, has $13.98 base and $0.69 fringe. A flat range reflects the available included lines, not a claim that every carpenter duty throughout North Carolina has one rate. The table can still show separate specialty work and named equipment. Review the contract's complete carpenter classification before using this figure for a task outside the general line or another construction type.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
