What these rates show

North Carolina residential electrical limits are survey rates with no hourly dollar fringe at either end. NC20260092, a decision covering Carteret County, gives $14.50 plus $0.00. NC20260101, a decision covering Forsyth County, gives $20.08 plus $0.00. Cash accounts for the difference between these quotes. This residential comparison is narrower than the survey-to-union electrical spread in the building set. Keep the construction source with the estimate so a building electrician allowance does not silently replace the residential rate assigned to the contract.

Residential plumbing is also an survey, zero-fringe comparison at its limits. The lower line at NC20260094, a decision covering Alleghany and 14 other counties, is $14.39 with $0.00 fringe. NC20260101, a decision covering Forsyth County, has $19.73 with $0.00. The high plumbing base is close to the high electrical base, but the lower sources differ. A county-group decision that supplies the lower plumbing line should not be assumed to supply every trade's lower rate. Read each work description before extending the residential crew's hours.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
