What these rates show

North Dakota highway electrical pay includes state-adopted and union lines inside a single broad decision. ND20260001, a decision covering Adams and 52 other counties, gives $40.00 plus $19.36 fringe as the state-adopted line. The upper union line in ND20260001, a decision covering Adams and 52 other counties, gives $55.35 plus $24.55. The source number alone will not distinguish them. Read the full electrical duties and local notes, especially before treating the broad county coverage as evidence that each rate applies uniformly across the whole decision.

The included highway carpenter base is flat within the state-adopted lines. ND20260001, a decision covering Adams and 52 other counties, lists $39.45 base with $9.75 fringe. That differs from the electrical range above, where source kinds and cash amounts vary. A flat carpenter base does not eliminate separate specialty duties or the need to match the contract line. Keep the printed carpenter scope with the road estimate, and use the full North Dakota highway schedule for work that is not represented by the common-trade rows.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
