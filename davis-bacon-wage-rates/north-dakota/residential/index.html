What these rates show

North Dakota residential electrical rates include a survey low and a union high with a long county restriction. ND20260002, a decision covering Ward County, has $12.001 plus $3.26 fringe. The union line in ND20260005 has $40.89 plus $17.91 for Adams,Billings,Bottineau,Bowman,Burke, Divide,Dunn,Emmons, Golden Valley, Hettinger,McHenry,McKenzie,McLean,Mercer,Mountrail, Oliver, Pierce,Renville,Rolette,Sheridan,Sioux,Slope,Stark. And Williams counties:. The upper cash base is more than three times the lower one. Keep the complete county list with the rate rather than shortening it to the first county named by the decision, which could give a different impression of its scope.

Roofing has a smaller residential spread and survey sources at both ends. ND20260003, a decision covering Burleigh and 2 other counties, gives $8.132 with $0.00 fringe; ND20260005, a decision covering Adams and 47 other counties, gives $11.613 with $0.00. Both fringe amounts are zero, unlike the electrical comparison above. A residential bid's trade mix will therefore affect how much the union electrical obligation changes the total. For roof hours, use the actual roofing classification and county-group source rather than extending the electrical wage pattern to the whole crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
