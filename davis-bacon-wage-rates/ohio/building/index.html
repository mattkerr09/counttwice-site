What these rates show

Ohio building carpentry has a clear survey-to-union split. The survey carpenter in OH20260086, a decision covering Licking County, receives $15.50 with $0.00 fringe. The union line at OH20260032, a decision covering Ashtabula County, receives $37.52 plus $23.50. The upper base is more than twice the lower one, with benefits adding substantially to the gap. These figures come from building decisions. Residential rates use separate schedules and are left out of this building comparison so they do not lower its apparent wage floor.

The building plumber range is wider in cash than the carpenter range. OH20260089, a decision covering Madison County, gives the survey plumber $21.04 plus $4.29 fringe. The union line in OH20260079, a decision covering Fulton County, gives $51.00 plus $32.56. Both components rise sharply. Electrical cable-puller and low-voltage-only lines are kept in separate specialty entries in the table.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
