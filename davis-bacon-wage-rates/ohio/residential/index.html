What these rates show

Ohio residential electrical limits include a union-average line and a union line. OH20260023, a decision covering Belmont County, gives the union-average base of $18.98 plus $10.14 fringe. OH20260010, a decision covering Hamilton County, gives the union rate of $38.05 plus $22.97. The upper cash amount is more than twice the lower one. Keep the source kinds distinct when comparing residential electrical costs, and use the exact line assigned to the contract rather than treating either end as a statewide rate.

Carpentry has survey rates at both residential limits and a different benefit pattern. OH20260013, a decision covering Richland County, lists $13.86 base with $0.83 fringe. OH20260010, a decision covering Hamilton County, lists $27.29 with $0.00. The higher cash line has zero fringe, while the lower line has a small benefit amount. That differs from the electrical comparison, where both fringes are substantial. Price the residential carpenter and electrical hours separately, keeping the printed pair from each chosen Ohio source in the estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
