What these rates show

Oklahoma building carpentry spans survey and union sources with a large cash difference. OK20260052, a decision covering Rogers County, has $12.381 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line in OK20260063, a decision covering Payne County, has $27.79 plus $10.05. The upper base is more than twice the lower base, and the higher line adds benefits. Establish the building decision early in the bid so a zero-fringe survey allowance from another Oklahoma project does not become the assumed cost for every carpenter hour.

Electrical rates show that different source kinds can appear within the same building decision. OK20260039, a decision covering Jackson County, gives the survey electrician $17.50 with $2.27 fringe. The union line in OK20260039, a decision covering Jackson County, gives $41.40 plus $13.14. Both quotes share a decision number, but their cash and benefits differ markedly. Read the actual electrical classification and local scope inside that document. Merely identifying the right county decision does not make its lower and upper electrician lines interchangeable.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
