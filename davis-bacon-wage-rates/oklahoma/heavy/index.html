What these rates show

Oklahoma heavy electrical pay has a wide survey-to-union spread. OK20260025, a decision covering Logan County, gives $22.00 plus $4.76 fringe. The union electrician at OK20260023, a decision covering Comanche County, gives $51.00 plus $23.80. The higher cash rate is more than twice the lower rate, with much greater fringe as well. A heavy bid with many electrical hours needs the assigned source before its labor allowance is fixed. A single Oklahoma skilled-trade rate would hide this difference.

The concrete comparison is also survey to union, but it has a larger proportional base change. The survey cement mason in OK20260025, a decision covering Logan County, is $12.491 with $1.23 fringe; the union line in OK20260023, a decision covering Comanche County, is $33.52 with $15.88. The upper base is well over twice the lower one, and benefits rise sharply. The concrete and electrical quotes share lower and upper decisions here, yet the trades still have different paired amounts and should keep separate calculations.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
