What these rates show

Oklahoma highway cement mason limits are survey rates with county notes on the lines themselves. OK20260018 gives $11.041 plus $0.00 fringe for Caddo County. The upper line in OK20260019 gives $14.72 plus $1.35 for Ottawa County. Only the higher pair includes a dollar fringe. Retain those local notes when pricing the road's concrete work. A decision's wider coverage could include places beyond the county named for this particular cement mason amount.

Highway carpenter limits also have explicit county notes and survey sources. OK20260020 lists $11.752 with $0.00 fringe for Hughes County; OK20260017 lists $14.82 with $0.00 for Rogers County. Both benefit amounts are zero, so their cash difference carries the comparison. The carpenter counties differ from the concrete counties above. Match each trade's own geography instead of assuming one county note or one source decision applies to every line used in the Oklahoma highway estimate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
