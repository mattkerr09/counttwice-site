What these rates show

Oklahoma residential plumber limits show a wide cash spread within survey rates. OK20260001, a decision covering Carter and 9 other counties, prints $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. OK20260073, a decision covering Cleveland County, prints $33.14 plus $0.00. The higher base is more than four times the lower one, while both fringes are zero. Survey does not mean a narrow or uniform residential wage range. The contract's county source and plumber duties need to be settled before a low survey allowance is extended across the crew's hours.

Residential electrical pay is an survey comparison as well, with a smaller spread than plumbing. The line at OK20260001, a decision covering Carter and 9 other counties, is $7.251 with $0.00 fringe. The line at OK20260074, a decision covering Oklahoma County, is $23.14 with $0.00. Both have zero benefits in these quotes. The upper electrical and plumbing sources differ, so the highest rate for one trade should not be assumed to locate the highest rate for the other. Review each residential source pair separately in the Oklahoma bid.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
