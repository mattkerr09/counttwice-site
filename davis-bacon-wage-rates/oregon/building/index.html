What these rates show

Oregon building carpentry has a survey low with zero fringe and a union high with substantial benefits. OR20260097, a decision covering Deschutes County, gives $25.69 plus $0.00. OR20260084, a decision covering Curry County, gives $52.62 plus $16.81. The upper cash base is more than twice the lower base. A building estimate needs the county-covered decision before using one carpenter allowance across projects. The union and survey distinction matters here, but the full carpenter work and area limits still decide whether a particular line fits the job.

Electrical benefits are sizeable at both ends of the Oregon building range. The survey line in OR20260002, a decision covering Baker County, carries $40.48 with $19.97 fringe. The union line in OR20260091, a decision covering Wasco County, carries $63.50 with $29.73. Its cash spread is smaller proportionally than the carpenter spread. A survey electrical rate should not be treated as a cash-only obligation. Preserve the benefit printed beside the chosen base when comparing mixed building crews or preparing the electrical labor item.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
