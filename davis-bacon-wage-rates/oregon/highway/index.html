What these rates show

Oregon highway electrical limits are union lines within one broad decision, with different local markers. OR20260001 prints $42.55 plus $19.86 fringe for Malheur County. The upper line in OR20260001 prints $63.50 plus $29.73 under Zone 1. A county note and a zone label are different restrictions, so keep each with its own amount. The zone definition in the full highway source is needed before locating that upper electrician rate on a road project.

Cement mason bases have a much narrower union range than electrical bases in the highway decision. OR20260001, a decision covering Baker and 35 other counties, has $46.13 with $20.31 fringe. The upper cement mason line in OR20260001, a decision covering Baker and 35 other counties, has $48.05 with $20.31. Their benefit amounts are equal, so cash accounts for this small spread. Read the complete concrete descriptions and local conditions even when the numbers are close. The general range does not replace the source's rules for choosing a named work line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
