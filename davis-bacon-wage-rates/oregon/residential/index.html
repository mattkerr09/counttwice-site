What these rates show

Oregon residential general laborer rates have a wide survey-to-union split. The survey line in OR20260021, a decision covering Benton and 1 other counties, has $10.081 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line at OR20260012, a decision covering Clackamas County, has $39.11 plus $17.30. The upper base is nearly four times the lower one, and benefits add a further obligation. A residential crew's actual tasks still need to be matched to the full laborer classification in the contract.

The carpenter range is even wider in proportional cash terms. OR20260009, a decision covering Baker and 6 other counties, lists the survey carpenter at $7.252 with $0.00 fringe; OR20260007, a decision covering Klamath County, lists the union carpenter at $36.27 with $14.03. The higher base is about five times the lower base. A low survey figure from a broad county-group source should not become a general Oregon residential allowance. Review the carpenter line's work and local scope before multiplying the bid's labor hours. The residential equipment examples separate loader and bulldozer groups, while truck examples name dump-truck work. Keep loading, earth-moving and hauling hours tied to those printed duties when costing an Oregon housing site.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
