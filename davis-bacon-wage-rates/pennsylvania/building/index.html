What these rates show

Pennsylvania building carpentry spans survey and union sources with a wide cash and benefit gap. PA20260073, a decision covering Adams County, lists $17.32 plus $6.08 fringe. The union line at PA20260078, a decision covering Bucks County, lists $46.65 plus $29.84. The upper base is more than twice the lower one, and benefits increase much more sharply in proportional terms. Choose the building determination before extending carpenter hours, since a source from another Pennsylvania county decision can carry a very different obligation.

Electrical rates have a larger building cash spread than carpentry. The survey electrician in PA20260057, a decision covering Juniata County, is $25.44 with $12.35 fringe. The upper union line in PA20260003, a decision covering Philadelphia County, is $75.05 with $47.02. The upper base is close to three times the lower base, with a substantial benefit amount added. The electrical high also exceeds the carpenter high in both components. Keep the two trades' paired obligations separate when comparing Pennsylvania building crew costs.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
