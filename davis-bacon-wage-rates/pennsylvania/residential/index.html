What these rates show

Pennsylvania residential plumbing has a particularly wide survey-to-union range. PA20260025, a decision covering Bradford and 4 other counties, prints $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line in PA20260109, a decision covering Delaware County, prints $73.73 plus $39.86. The upper cash base is more than ten times the lower one, before benefits. A residential label is therefore no guide to the wage level by itself. Establish the assigned determination before using a low survey rate to cost the plumbing item or compare subcontractor labor allowances.

Electrical rates also span a low survey line and a much higher union obligation. PA20260026, a decision covering Blair and 1 other counties, has $8.802 with $0.00 fringe. PA20260114, a decision covering Philadelphia County, has $75.05 with $47.02. The upper electrical cash base is slightly above the upper plumber base, and its fringe is larger too. Those differences matter in a residential bid with both crews. Keep each trade's source pair intact rather than using one skilled-trade base or benefit amount across the Pennsylvania project.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
