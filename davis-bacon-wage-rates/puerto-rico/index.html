What these rates show

Puerto Rico's page combines building, heavy, highway and residential decisions, so this range compares different construction schedules. The survey electrician in PR20260001, a decision covering Adjuntas and 77 other counties, receives $7.25 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line in PR20260036, a decision covering Aguada and 16 other counties, receives $39.81 plus $14.62. The upper cash base is more than five times the lower one. Choose the contract's construction type before using this comparison; a broad building source and a local schedule for another type are not interchangeable.

Carpentry has a smaller survey spread across the combined schedules. PR20260001, a decision covering Adjuntas and 77 other counties, lists $7.25 base with $0.34 fringe. PR20260026, a decision covering Ponce, lists $13.07 with $0.99. Both quoted carpenter fringes are modest, unlike the upper electrical fringe above. Several Puerto Rico decisions cover many named areas, while others are more local. Use the full area list and work type in the chosen document, rather than treating its first place name as the location of every carpenter rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
