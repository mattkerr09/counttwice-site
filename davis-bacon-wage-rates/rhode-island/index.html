What these rates show

Rhode Island has a combined page because its few decisions overlap several construction types. Across those schedules, the survey electrician at RI20260003, a decision covering Newport County, has $18.49 plus $3.92 fringe. The union line at RI20260002, a decision covering Bristol and 3 other counties, has $42.09 plus $17.92. The upper base is more than twice the lower one. This is a comparison across schedules, not two rates to choose between for one contract. Settle the project type and decision before pricing electrical hours.

Plumbing also changes substantially across the combined Rhode Island sources. RI20260003, a decision covering Newport County, gives the survey plumber $23.69 with $8.76 fringe; RI20260001, a decision covering Bristol and 4 other counties, gives the union plumber $54.34 with $34.50. The higher fringe is a large addition to cash wages. Its amount is also greater than the high electrical fringe above. Keep the plumbing pair separate in the estimate, and review the construction coverage in the source before carrying a rate from one Rhode Island project to another.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
