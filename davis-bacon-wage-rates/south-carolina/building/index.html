What these rates show

South Carolina building carpenter limits both come from survey rates, yet the cash gap is large. SC20260009, a decision covering Chester County, gives $14.46 plus $0.78 fringe. SC20260002, a decision covering Aiken and 2 other counties, gives $35.47 plus $16.59. The upper base is more than twice the lower base and carries much more fringe. Survey is therefore not shorthand for one low statewide building price. Read the construction coverage and full work limits of the contract decision before using the upper or lower carpenter allowance.

Electrical work has an survey building comparison too. The lower line at SC20260009, a decision covering Chester County, is $15.00 with $0.00 fringe; the upper line at SC20260002, a decision covering Aiken and 2 other counties, is $38.75 with $18.23. Only the upper pair has a dollar fringe here. The same broad source supplies the high carpenter and electrician quotes, but those trades have different amounts. Keep the electrical duties and any narrower area notes with the source rather than using the broad decision's first county as the rate location.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
