What these rates show

South Carolina heavy carpenter rates can differ by several times within survey sources. SC20260001, a decision covering Abbeville and 24 other counties, prints $7.421 plus $0.00 fringe. SC20260002, a decision covering Aiken and 2 other counties, prints $35.47 plus $16.59. The upper cash base is nearly five times the lower one, and its fringe adds another substantial cost. A union-to-survey explanation would miss the point here because both ends are survey lines. Match the heavy decision and complete carpenter work limits before carrying an allowance across projects.

The concrete comparison has another wide survey spread. The cement mason in SC20260001, a decision covering Abbeville and 24 other counties, receives $7.251 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line at SC20260002, a decision covering Aiken and 2 other counties, receives $30.74 with $17.31. Its base is more than four times the lower one. The upper concrete base is below the upper carpenter base, while its fringe is slightly larger. For a heavy crew using both trades, the paired obligations matter more than one shared wage or benefit assumption.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
