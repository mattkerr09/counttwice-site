What these rates show

South Carolina highway general laborer limits are survey rates with very different paired costs. SC20260039, a decision covering Abbeville and 13 other counties, gives $13.081 plus $0.00 fringe. SC20260040, a decision covering Allendale and 10 other counties, gives $18.67 plus $0.00. The upper cash rate is higher while both sources list zero fringe. The lower line covers a county group. The upper line names one county. Review the contract's actual work and local restrictions before treating a broad coverage heading as one general laborer rate for every road location.

Concrete has a smaller proportional cash spread, but a large benefit change in the highway survey lines. SC20260039, a decision covering Abbeville and 13 other counties, lists $17.19 base with $0.00 fringe; SC20260002, a decision covering Aiken and 2 other counties, lists $30.74 with $17.31. The benefit increase is larger than the cash increase between these quotes. That makes the fringe central to a concrete-heavy road bid. Keep the cement mason pair separate from the general laborer pair, even when the same decisions supply both comparisons.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
