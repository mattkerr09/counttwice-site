What these rates show

South Dakota building carpenter limits are survey lines with relatively close cash amounts. SD20260014, a decision covering Harding County, lists $15.29 plus $2.55 fringe; SD20260029, a decision covering Pennington County, lists $19.78 plus $4.11. Benefits are due at both ends and rise with the base. A survey source should not be read as a zero-fringe source. Use the building county decision before extending carpenter hours, even though the difference here is much smaller than the plumbing comparison in the same state.

Plumbing has a survey low and a union high with a much larger fringe. SD20260004, a decision covering Aurora County, gives $14.02 with $1.90 fringe. The union plumber at SD20260008, a decision covering Butte County, gives $34.90 plus $15.70. The higher base is well over twice the lower base, and the benefit increase adds a large cost. This plumber source mix differs from the carpenter survey range. Cost the building trades separately and keep the plumber's printed base and benefit obligation paired.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
