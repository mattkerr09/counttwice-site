What these rates show

South Dakota's included heavy and highway electrician range has one state-adopted base. SD20260001, a decision covering Aurora and 65 other counties, gives $35.43 plus $8.69 fringe. This heavy and highway set does not supply general carpenter, plumber or cement mason ranges under the matching rules used here. Missing rows should not be filled with a number borrowed from a building schedule. Start with the heavy and highway decision and its complete classifications, then find the actual crew duties in that source before setting an allowance.

The included state-adopted operator lines do have a cash spread. SD20260001, a decision covering Aurora and 65 other counties, lists $30.16 base with $0.00 fringe, while SD20260001, a decision covering Aurora and 65 other counties, lists $34.96 with $8.36. Only the upper pair has a dollar fringe. Equipment descriptions decide which line fits the work, so these are not interchangeable operator prices. Read the full South Dakota heavy and highway equipment schedule before pricing a machine or assigning a worker to an operator classification.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
