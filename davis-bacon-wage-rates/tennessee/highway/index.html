What these rates show

Tennessee highway carpentry has one included general survey base in this source set. TN20260147, a decision covering Anderson and 94 other counties, lists $17.52 plus $0.00 fringe. The table therefore shows a flat base range, rather than a county-to-county carpenter spread. The source covers a broad list of counties, so the quote is described through decision coverage. Read any work and local limits in the highway document before using the amount, especially when a crew estimate was carried forward from a building project.

The general electrical range is flat too. The survey electrician at TN20260147, a decision covering Anderson and 94 other counties, has $24.08 base with $0.00 fringe. Both quoted highway trades have zero fringe, but their cash bases differ. Keep the electrician and carpenter classifications distinct for estimating and payroll. A flat range only describes the included general lines; it does not make every equipment group or electrical specialty the same price. Use the full Tennessee highway source for duties beyond the short common-trade table.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
