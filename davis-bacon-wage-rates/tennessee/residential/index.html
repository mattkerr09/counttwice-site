What these rates show

Tennessee residential cement mason limits are survey lines with no dollar fringe at either end. TN20260002, a decision covering Cocke County, gives $10.001 plus $0.00; TN20260017, a decision covering Chester County, gives $16.00 plus $0.00. The cash difference accounts for the comparison between these concrete quotes. These residential amounts belong to their own construction schedules, so keep the decision with the bid rather than carrying a heavy or building concrete assumption onto a residential crew without reviewing the contract source.

Carpentry has an survey residential range too, but the upper line adds a fringe obligation. TN20260003, a decision covering Crockett County, lists $12.212 with $0.00 fringe. TN20260018, a decision covering Davidson County, lists $18.09 with $2.07. The upper carpenter cash base exceeds the upper concrete base, and the benefit pattern differs as well. Those trade differences matter even in a modest residential estimate. Use the carpenter line's actual pair instead of applying the concrete rows' zero-fringe pattern to every worker on the project.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
