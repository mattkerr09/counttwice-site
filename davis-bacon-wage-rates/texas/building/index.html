What these rates show

Texas building bids can cross a large survey-to-union gap. The survey carpenter in TX20260156, a decision covering Howard County, has $12.421 base and $0.00 fringe. The union carpenter in TX20260229, a decision covering Austin County, has $33.27 plus $9.73. The upper base is well over twice the lower one, before benefits. A single Texas carpenter allowance would conceal that difference. Check the building decision for the job, then the duties and any area limits on the carpenter line.

Plumbing has a wider cash spread than carpentry in these building sources. TX20260121, a decision covering Brooks County, prints a survey base of $12.502 with $0.00 fringe. The union line at TX20260157, a decision covering Hudspeth County, prints $40.74 with $15.35. Its cash base is more than three times the lower one and its fringe adds a substantial cost. Both quoted trades have zero fringe at the survey end. Check the fringe on the upper union lines and each Texas survey line.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
