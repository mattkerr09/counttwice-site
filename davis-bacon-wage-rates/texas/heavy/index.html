What these rates show

Texas heavy plumbing spans a low survey line and a union line with a base more than three times as high. TX20260058, a decision covering Grayson County, lists $10.961 plus $0.00 fringe. TX20260016, a decision covering Denton County, lists $39.76 plus $14.04. The upper line adds benefits where the lower pair has none. Establish the heavy decision before costing plumbing hours, since a general Texas labor allowance can miss both the cash and fringe differences between these printed sources.

Electrical pay follows a similar heavy source split with a different fringe amount. The survey electrician at TX20260058, a decision covering Grayson County, is $11.431 with $0.00 fringe; the union line at TX20260016, a decision covering Denton County, is $39.75 with $11.67. The high electrical and plumbing bases are almost equal, but the high plumbing fringe is larger. That small base difference should not lead an estimator to merge the trades into one total rate. Keep each obligation paired with its own Texas heavy classification.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
