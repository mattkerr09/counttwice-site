What these rates show

Texas highway electrical limits here are state-adopted rates with zero fringe at both ends. TX20260046, a decision covering Archer and 15 other counties, gives $23.70 plus $0.00. TX20260294, a decision covering Collin and 12 other counties, gives $36.10 plus $0.00. The cash spread is sizeable despite the common source kind and benefit pattern. These highway decisions cover county groups. Read the full road-contract source instead of carrying a building union rate or treating the first county in a coverage group as the individual electrical line's location.

The highway general laborer comparison is narrower in cash and also uses state-adopted lines. TX20260051, a decision covering Cameron and 2 other counties, prints $14.88 with $0.00 fringe; TX20260298, a decision covering Andrews and 81 other counties, prints $18.10 with $0.00. Both benefit amounts are zero. That does not make the laborer and electrical costs interchangeable, and their county-group sources differ. Check the actual laborer duties in the contract decision.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
