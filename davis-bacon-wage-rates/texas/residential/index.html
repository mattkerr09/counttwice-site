What these rates show

Texas residential electrical rates include a zero-fringe survey line and a much higher union line with benefits. TX20260001, a decision covering Cameron and 1 other counties, lists $7.251 plus $0.00. TX20260012, a decision covering Hardin and 2 other counties, lists $33.76 plus $13.56. The upper cash base is more than four times the lower one. A residential designation does not establish the wage level. Determine which county-group schedule belongs to the contract before using a low survey electrical allowance for the whole project.

Plumbing shows a different source pattern from electricians: both residential limits are survey lines with zero fringe. The plumber at TX20260010, a decision covering Nueces and 1 other counties, is $7.252 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line at TX20260092, a decision covering Cass County, is $20.00 with $0.00. The upper cash base is still well over twice the lower base. Texas residential cost differences therefore are not all union-to-survey differences. Keep the plumbing source and its actual duties separate from the electrical comparison when pricing a mixed crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
