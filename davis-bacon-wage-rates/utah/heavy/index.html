What these rates show

Utah heavy electrical work has survey and union lines with benefits at both ends. UT20260011, a decision covering Summit County, lists $23.74 plus $7.64 fringe; UT20260090, a decision covering Cache County, lists $41.23 plus $17.66. The base increase is the larger part of the difference, though benefits also change materially. Match the heavy county decision and electrical duties before using an allowance from another Utah project. The source kind helps explain the range, but it does not replace the line's work and area limits.

General laborer rates cross from a zero-fringe survey line to a union line whose base is more than twice as high. UT20260019, a decision covering Iron County, has $9.001 with $0.00 fringe. UT20260011, a decision covering Summit County, has $20.59 with $8.65. The added benefit amount matters to a labor-heavy estimate. Natural gas pipeline construction has its own stringer-truck description in the truck details. Match pipeline hauling to that named task when checking a heavy estimate, and keep the operator's full work restriction with the planned machine.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
