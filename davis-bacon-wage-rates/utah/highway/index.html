What these rates show

Utah highway electrician rates move from a survey line with modest fringe to a union line with a much larger fringe. UT20260110, a decision covering Washington County, gives $26.72 plus $2.30. UT20260105, a decision covering Cache County, gives $41.23 plus $17.77. The benefit increase is slightly larger than the cash increase between these quotes. A cash-only highway comparison would therefore miss a large share of the difference. Keep the electrical pair and its county decision with the road estimate.

The general laborer highway comparison stays within survey lines. UT20260010, a decision covering Grand County, prints $13.371 with $2.65 fringe, while UT20260101, a decision covering Wasatch County, prints $24.31 with $8.65. Both components increase at the upper end. This is a local survey spread, unlike the electrical survey-to-union change above. An estimate with both trades needs separate source checks and benefit calculations. The work description still decides which laborer line fits.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
