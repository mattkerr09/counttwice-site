What these rates show

Utah residential concrete work includes a survey line with zero fringe and a union line with a base more than five times as high. UT20260003, a decision covering Box Elder County, has $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. UT20260035, a decision covering Summit County, has $36.66 plus $11.25. Benefits add another large cost to the upper cash figure. Resolve the assigned residential decision before pricing the concrete crew; a low survey allowance from another Utah county source can be far below the applicable union obligation.

Plumbing has a similarly wide residential source split, though the upper base is below the upper concrete base. The survey plumber in UT20260005, a decision covering Rich County, receives $7.252 with $0.00 fringe. The union line in UT20260030, a decision covering Cache County, receives $30.60 with $10.85. The higher cash rate is more than four times the lower one. The two trades' upper fringes are close but not equal. Keep each printed pair rather than sharing a single skilled-trade benefit amount across the Utah residential crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
