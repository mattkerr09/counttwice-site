What these rates show

Vermont building carpentry has a large survey-to-union change in both cash and fringe. VT20260019, a decision covering Orleans County, lists $14.00 plus $3.82 fringe. The union line at VT20260017, a decision covering Bennington County, lists $32.39 plus $23.43. The upper base is more than twice the lower base, and benefits grow much more sharply in proportional terms. Select the building county decision before setting the carpenter allowance, because a cash-only comparison would leave out a large part of the difference between these sources.

The plumbing range is survey to union as well, but benefits are already sizeable at its lower base. VT20260016, a decision covering Addison County, gives $24.04 with $8.25 fringe; VT20260025, a decision covering Chittenden County, gives $37.40 with $19.02. The upper plumber cash base exceeds the upper carpenter base, while its fringe is smaller. A mixed Vermont building crew therefore needs trade-specific totals. Copy the pair from the applicable plumbing line rather than taking the largest base and largest fringe from different table entries.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
