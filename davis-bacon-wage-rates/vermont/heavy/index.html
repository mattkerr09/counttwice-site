What these rates show

Vermont heavy general laborer rates have a survey low with very little fringe and a union high with a substantial fringe. VT20260068, a decision covering Franklin County, prints $13.69 plus $0.16. VT20260066, a decision covering Addison and 6 other counties, prints $25.40 plus $21.43. The benefit increase is larger than the cash increase. For a labor-heavy bid, check the full pair before comparing sources.

Electrical cash pay is much closer across the heavy survey lines, and the fringe moves in the opposite direction. The lower-base line at VT20260066, a decision covering Addison and 6 other counties, has $24.13 with $11.96 fringe. The upper-base line at VT20260067, a decision covering Chittenden County, has $28.82 with $2.85. The higher base's fringe is much smaller. That means a larger cash number does not necessarily mean a larger paired obligation. Use the complete electrical pair rather than drawing a cost conclusion from the Vermont heavy base range alone.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
