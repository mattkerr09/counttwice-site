What these rates show

Vermont highway carpenter limits are survey lines with small fringes at both ends. VT20260054, a decision covering Orleans County, gives $19.00 plus $0.74 fringe; VT20260056, a decision covering Washington County, gives $24.11 plus $0.64. The upper cash line has a slightly smaller fringe. Keep each pair together when comparing road carpenter costs. These highway sources have a different pattern from the wider building union-to-survey range, so a rate carried from a building estimate should not silently replace the road contract's assigned line.

General laborer pay has a smaller survey highway cash spread. VT20260048, a decision covering Addison County, lists $15.87 with $3.07 fringe. VT20260049, a decision covering Bennington County, lists $18.63 with $3.92. Here both components increase in the upper quote, unlike the carpenter ends above. Use the laborer's own source pair for its hours rather than applying a shared Vermont road-crew uplift.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
