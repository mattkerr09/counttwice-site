What these rates show

Vermont residential cement mason limits show a small survey cash spread with zero fringe at both ends. VT20260002, a decision covering Rutland County, has $7.251 plus $0.00; VT20260009, a decision covering Chittenden County, has $8.702 plus $0.00. The source difference is modest, unlike the much wider building concrete and carpenter patterns. Use the residential determination in the contract before extending concrete hours. A short range still represents different source lines and does not create one statewide rate for every residential site.

Plumbing has an even smaller residential cash difference. The survey plumber at VT20260001, a decision covering Washington County, receives $7.253 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line at VT20260002, a decision covering Rutland County, receives $7.511 with $0.00. Both benefit amounts are zero. The cash bases are close enough that the work and county source deserve more attention than the size of the range. Keep the plumber and concrete classifications separate for payroll, even when a residential estimate uses similar-looking survey amounts for both trades.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
