What these rates show

The Virgin Islands page combines building, heavy, highway and residential schedules across the listed island areas. The survey electrician in VI20260001, a decision covering St Croix and 2 other counties, has $7.63 plus $0.00 fringe. The upper survey line at VI20260003, a decision covering St Croix, has $39.25 plus $0.00. Its cash base is more than five times the lower base, despite zero fringe in both quotes. This is a comparison across schedules. The contract's construction type and island coverage must be resolved before using either electrical rate.

Plumbing also varies widely across the combined survey sources. VI20260001, a decision covering St Croix and 2 other counties, prints $7.25 base with $0.00 fringe. VI20260006, a decision covering St Croix, prints $31.75 with $0.00. The upper base is more than four times the lower one, again with zero fringe at both limits. The high plumbing and high electrical amounts come from different decisions. Choose the source assigned to the actual project, rather than treating an island name alone as enough to select a rate from this combined table.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
