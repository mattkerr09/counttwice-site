What these rates show

Virginia building carpentry ranges from survey pay with modest fringe to union pay with a much larger fringe. VA20260172, a decision covering Botetourt and 5 other counties, gives $13.491 plus $1.10. VA20260158, a decision covering Loudoun County, gives $35.89 plus $14.44. The higher cash base is well over twice the lower base. Some Virginia sources list counties and independent cities together, so read the full printed coverage. The first name in that list is not used here as the carpenter rate's own geographic restriction.

Electrical pay has a larger cash spread than carpentry. The survey line at VA20260014, a decision covering Augusta and 2 other counties, carries $19.52 with $5.04 fringe. The union electrician in VA20260013, a decision covering Arlington and 1 other counties, carries $59.50 with $23.06. The upper base is more than three times the lower base. Its benefit amount also rises substantially. A Virginia building estimate with both crews should keep their source pairs separate, and review the original place names and duties before applying a rate to county or city work.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
