What these rates show

Virginia heavy electrical work shows a wide survey-to-union spread. VA20260001, a decision covering Accomack and 135 other counties, lists $13.451 plus $2.96 fringe; VA20260004, a decision covering Arlington and 1 other counties, lists $59.50 plus $23.06. The upper cash base is more than four times the lower base, with sizeable benefits added. The lower source covers many county and city names, so a short first-county label would be misleading. Use the heavy decision's full coverage and any narrower electrical note before setting the labor allowance.

Concrete has a survey-to-union change too, with a smaller cash spread than electricians. The cement mason in VA20260026, a decision covering Brunswick and 10 other counties, receives $11.002 with $1.02 fringe. The union line in VA20260004, a decision covering Arlington and 1 other counties, receives $30.50 plus $14.23. The upper base is well over twice the lower one. Both components matter when comparing a concrete-heavy job with another Virginia heavy project. Preserve the trade's paired obligation instead of using a general skilled-trade benefit amount or a county label inferred from the document heading.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
