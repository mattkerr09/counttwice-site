What these rates show

Virginia highway general laborer limits include survey and union lines. VA20260101, a decision covering Amherst County, prints $12.821 plus $0.00 fringe. The union line in VA20260103, a decision covering Arlington and 1 other counties, prints $23.06 plus $8.74. The lower pair has zero fringe while the upper adds a meaningful benefit amount. Check the contract source before extending road labor hours. County and independent-city coverage can appear together.

The cement mason highway comparison stays within survey lines and has zero fringes at both ends. VA20260100, a decision covering Amelia County, gives $16.03 with $0.00 fringe; VA20260179, a decision covering Loudoun County, gives $21.64 with $0.00. Its cash spread is smaller than the general laborer spread above. That difference in source kind and benefits matters to a road estimate with both crews. Use the concrete line's own source and full duties rather than extending the union laborer fringe pattern to the entire Virginia highway workforce.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
