What these rates show

Virginia residential carpenter limits are survey rates, with zero fringe at the lower base and benefits at the upper base. VA20260128, a decision covering Wise and 1 other counties, has $12.061 plus $0.00. VA20260136, a decision covering Dinwiddie and 1 other counties, has $21.44 plus $3.44. Both documents include county and city coverage, so read their complete place lists and any local notes on the carpenter line. A residential estimate needs that source detail even without a union line at either end of the comparison.

Concrete shows a narrower survey residential spread. The cement mason in VA20260135, a decision covering Chesapeake*, is $15.04 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line in VA20260134, a decision covering Arlington and 1 other counties, is $20.13 with $1.00. The upper concrete base is below the upper carpenter base, and its fringe is smaller as well. Those trade differences should remain visible in the bid. Use each paired line for its own hours rather than treating a Virginia residential survey source as one common crew rate.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
