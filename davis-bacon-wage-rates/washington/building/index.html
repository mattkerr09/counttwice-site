What these rates show

Washington building carpentry has a survey low and a union high with a base well over twice as large. WA20260035, a decision covering Okanogan County, lists $23.81 plus $2.91 fringe. WA20260022, a decision covering Clallam County, lists $63.98 plus $19.13. Benefits widen the gap beyond the cash difference. Match the county-covered building decision before using a carpenter allowance across Washington projects. The source kind explains part of the spread, but the work and any local limits on the exact line still have to fit the crew.

Plumbing has a wider building cash spread than carpentry and a particularly large upper fringe. The survey plumber at WA20260021, a decision covering Adams County, receives $35.00 with $0.00 fringe; the union line at WA20260022, a decision covering Clallam County, receives $75.21 with $33.88. Only the upper pair has a dollar fringe here. The high plumbing base and fringe both exceed the high carpenter pair. Keep those different obligations separate when checking whether a mixed building crew's allowance covers the Washington contract rates.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
