What these rates show

Washington heavy carpenter rates span survey and union sources with a large change in cash. WA20260052, a decision covering Lincoln County, gives $24.03 plus $6.50 fringe; WA20260044, a decision covering Clallam County, gives $63.98 plus $19.13. The upper base is well over twice the lower base, and the fringe also rises. Establish the heavy decision before extending carpenter hours. A source from another construction type or county decision can look familiar while carrying a very different paired wage obligation.

Concrete has a larger proportional heavy cash spread than carpentry. The survey cement mason in WA20260043, a decision covering Adams County, has $17.21 with $0.00 fringe. The union line in WA20260044, a decision covering Clallam County, has $54.16 with $21.27. The high cash base is more than three times the low base, with benefits added where the survey pair has none. The upper concrete base is below the upper carpenter base, but its fringe is larger. Use each printed pair rather than sharing a single heavy-crew benefit allowance.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
