What these rates show

Washington highway electrical limits are union lines with county groups printed beside them. WA20260001 gives $42.45 plus $21.34 fringe for Chelan, Douglas, Grant and Okanogan counties. The upper line in WA20260001 gives $75.96 plus $33.48 for Callam, Jefferson, King and Kitsap counties. Both cash and benefits rise substantially. The source's county wording is retained as printed, including its spelling. Read the full decision before placing the road in a group, rather than using the first county in the broad coverage list as the electrician rate's location.

Carpenter geography differs from the electrical groups within the same highway source. WA20260001 lists $44.21 base with $17.28 fringe for Adams, Asotin, Benton, Chelan, Columbia, Douglas, Ferry, Franklin, Garfield, Grant, Kittitas, Lincoln, Okanogan, Pend Oreille, Spokane, Stevens, Walla Walla, Whitman, and Yakima counties. Its upper line in WA20260001 lists $63.98 with $19.13 for King and Kitsap counties. The fringe change is much smaller than the cash change. A road crew needs the carpenter's own county list, not the electrical list above. Keep those separate local restrictions and paired amounts in the estimate even though both trades share a Washington highway decision number.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
