What these rates show

Washington residential general laborer rates have a wide survey-to-union split. WA20260100, a decision covering Columbia and 2 other counties, prints $11.871 plus $0.00 fringe. WA20260081, a decision covering Clallam County, prints $50.38 plus $16.96. The higher cash base is more than four times the lower one, and benefits add another obligation.

Concrete has a smaller cash ratio but a large benefit change across the residential survey and union limits. The survey cement mason in WA20260081, a decision covering Clallam County, is $27.83 with $0.00 fringe. The union line in WA20260091, a decision covering King County, is $54.16 with $21.27. The upper concrete base and fringe both exceed the upper general laborer pair. A residential estimate with many concrete hours will respond differently from a mostly laborer crew, so preserve the trade-specific amounts rather than using one Washington crew allowance. Residential equipment details name bobcat, skid-steer and skid-loader work alongside a separate backhoe, excavator and trackhoe description. Check those machine groups when costing site preparation before assigning a general laborer allowance to the crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
