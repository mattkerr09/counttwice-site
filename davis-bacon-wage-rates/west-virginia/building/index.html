What these rates show

West Virginia building carpenter limits are union rates with benefits due at both ends. WV20260046, a decision covering Preston County, gives $24.76 plus $18.39 fringe. WV20260006, a decision covering Fayette County, gives $33.84 plus $25.51. The base spread is moderate compared with some survey-to-union states, but the benefit amounts remain a large part of each paired obligation. Match the building county decision before using a carpenter allowance from another project, and read the full work description for specialty duties outside the common range.

Electrical work has a survey low and a union high, unlike the union carpenter comparison. The survey line at WV20260004, a decision covering Braxton County, carries $28.16 with $15.11 fringe. The union line at WV20260005, a decision covering Doddridge County, carries $43.77 with $27.72. Both components increase substantially. A survey electrical source still carries meaningful benefits, so it should not be priced as cash only. Keep the electrical source pair separate from the carpenter pair when assessing a mixed West Virginia building crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
