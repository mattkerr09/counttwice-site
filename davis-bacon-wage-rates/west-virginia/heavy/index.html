What these rates show

West Virginia heavy electrical pay includes survey and union sources with substantial fringe at both limits. WV20260066, a decision covering Cabell County, lists $26.38 plus $17.06 fringe; WV20260071, a decision covering Monongalia County, lists $43.77 plus $27.72. Cash and benefits both rise at the upper end. Determine the heavy county source before extending electrical hours. A low survey base does not mean benefits are minor, and a union label does not identify one uniform electrical obligation across the state's heavy decisions.

Carpentry has a smaller heavy cash spread than electrical work, but its benefit increase is sizeable. The survey carpenter in WV20260066, a decision covering Cabell County, receives $25.40 with $11.34 fringe. The union line at WV20260063, a decision covering Berkeley County, receives $36.05 plus $19.86. The lower carpenter fringe is below the lower electrical fringe, and their upper amounts differ too. Price each trade from its own complete line rather than applying a shared benefit percentage to a West Virginia heavy crew.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
