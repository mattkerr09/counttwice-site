What these rates show

West Virginia highway cement mason limits compare survey and union lines, with large benefits at both ends. WV20260101, a decision covering Clay County, gives $28.67 plus $18.85 fringe; WV20260081, a decision covering Barbour County, gives $35.77 plus $27.54. The benefit increase is larger than the base increase. A road estimate focused only on cash wages would miss much of the paired cost change. Check the county decision and complete concrete duties before carrying a cement mason allowance from one highway project to another.

Highway carpentry also crosses survey and union sources, with a relatively small base spread beside sizeable fringes. The lower line in WV20260088, a decision covering Mercer County, is $27.72 with $19.44 fringe. The upper line in WV20260083, a decision covering Greenbrier County, is $33.80 with $27.34. Here too, benefits rise by more than cash. The carpenter amounts remain different from the concrete pair above, so keep separate calculations for those road crews and use the exact source line for each classification's hours.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
