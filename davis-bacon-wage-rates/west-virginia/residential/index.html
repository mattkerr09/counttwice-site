What these rates show

West Virginia residential roofing limits are survey lines with a base spread of more than two to one. WV20260003, a decision covering Berkeley and 1 other counties, lists $7.251 plus $0.00 fringe. WV20260001, a decision covering Brooke and 9 other counties, lists $15.27 plus $3.20. Only the upper pair has a dollar fringe. The sources cover county groups rather than one job site, so these are described through decision coverage. Read the full roofer line and residential contract source before carrying the low survey amount across projects.

Plumbing has a smaller survey residential base spread and zero fringes at both limits. The plumber at WV20260003, a decision covering Berkeley and 1 other counties, has $7.251 with $0.00 fringe. The upper line at WV20260002, a decision covering Barbour and 42 other counties, has $11.502 with $0.00. The lower roofing and plumbing quotes share a source, but their upper sources differ. A county group with the higher roofing amount should not be assumed to have the higher plumber amount as well. Check each residential classification and paired rate separately.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
