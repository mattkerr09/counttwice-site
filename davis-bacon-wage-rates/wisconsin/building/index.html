What these rates show

Wisconsin building carpenter limits are union lines with a fairly close fringe pair despite a noticeable cash spread. WI20260007, a decision covering Douglas County, prints $40.79 plus $27.90 fringe. WI20260011, Burnett (west of highway 48) and Polk(west of highways 35, 48 & 65) counties, prints $48.54 plus $28.84. Benefits are substantial at both ends. The upper source covers a broad county group, so its first county is not used as the carpenter rate's own location. Read the complete classification and any local note inside the decision before pricing the building crew.

Electrical cash pay has a wider building spread than carpentry, while both ends remain union rates. WI20260002, a decision covering Marathon County, gives $40.19 with $22.90 fringe; WI20260001, a decision covering Milwaukee and 3 other counties, gives $50.86 with $28.26. The upper electrical base exceeds the upper carpenter base, but its fringe is slightly smaller. That difference is easy to lose in a shared skilled-trade allowance. Keep the source pair for each trade and review its local scope, rather than combining the largest numbers from Wisconsin building table columns.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
