What these rates show

Wisconsin residential electrical rates have a wide cash spread within union sources. WI20260020 gives $19.62 plus $10.72 fringe with the restriction Marinette (remainder of county), Menominee (east of a line 6 miles west of the west boundary of Oconto County), and Shawano (except Aniwa & Hutchins) counties. The upper line in WI20260026, a decision covering Kenosha County, gives $50.01 plus $28.40. The lower boundary is defined by a line measured from a county boundary, not simply by a county name. Preserve that direction and distance when locating the work. The first county in the broad decision would not describe the lower electrician rate's actual stated area.

Plumbing has a narrower residential base ratio and fringes that fall slightly as the quoted cash base rises. WI20260020, Marinette County (Niagara only), lists $36.51 with $28.43 fringe. The upper union line in WI20260020 lists $60.57 with $27.34 for Marquette County. Its county note is attached to that line. Compare the printed pairs, rather than adding the lower line's larger fringe to the upper base. The plumber geography also needs its own review instead of borrowing the electrician's boundary rule.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
