What these rates show

Wyoming's page combines construction schedules, so the carpenter range compares different project types. The survey line in WY20260003, a decision covering Albany and 20 other counties, gives $11.251 plus $0.75 fringe. The state-adopted line at WY20260043, a decision covering Natrona County, gives $37.99 plus $17.12. The upper cash base is more than three times the lower one. Choose the contract's construction schedule before using this comparison, since residential, building and road-related decisions can have different obligations even when they cover overlapping Wyoming county groups.

Plumbing also moves from a survey source to a state-adopted source across the combined schedules. WY20260003, a decision covering Albany and 20 other counties, prints $14.28 base with $0.80 fringe; WY20260041, a decision covering Albany and 22 other counties, prints $39.95 with $11.70. The upper plumber cash base exceeds the upper carpenter base, while its fringe is smaller. Keep the trade pairs separate when comparing Wyoming project costs. The source list shows the available decisions, but their construction coverage and complete duties still determine which plumber line belongs to the contract.

How to use these rates

Pay the rate for the work actually done, whatever the worker's skill. A worker who does two trades can be paid each trade's rate for its hours only if the payroll records accurately show the time spent in each (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(i)).

The basic hourly rate is the cash wage. The fringe is a second amount owed for every hour. It can go into bona fide benefit plans, be paid in cash on top of the base, or be a mix, as long as the total is at least base plus fringe (40 U.S.C. 3142(d); 29 CFR 5.31(b)).

A trade the work needs but the decision doesn't list is added only through the contract's conformance process (29 CFR 5.5(a)(1)(iii)).
