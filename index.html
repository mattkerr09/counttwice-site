An overtime example

Say the base rate is $30.00 and the fringe is $10.00, paid in cash. An overtime hour needs at least $30.00 times 1.5, which is $45.00, plus the $10.00 cash fringe. That's $55.00. Paying 1.5 times $40.00, or $60.00, is fine too. It's just more than the minimum.

One catch: the page goes by your columns. If you pay a single $40.00 rate and put all of it under rate_paid, it figures overtime on $40.00. If $10.00 of that is cash in place of fringe, put the $10.00 under cash_fringe. Then overtime is figured on $30.00.

Know the limits

A check of your numbers. The judgment is yours.

What it checks

  • Base plus fringe. Every hour needs at least the base rate plus the fringe. You can pay it as cash, as plan payments, or both, and cash above the base rate counts toward the fringe. Sources: 29 CFR 5.31 and 40 U.S.C. 3142(d).
  • Overtime. Hours over 40 in the week need at least 1.5 times the basic rate, and the fringe is added at its straight amount. The basic rate is never lower than the base rate on your wage determination. Sources: 40 U.S.C. 3702, 40 U.S.C. 3142(e) and 29 CFR 5.32.
  • Apprentices. On any day, a trade's apprentices can't outnumber what the program's ratio allows. An apprentice over the ratio gets the full rate for the work. Source: 29 CFR 5.5(a)(4).
  • Your file and your rates. Rows it can't read and trades with no rate typed are listed, so nothing gets skipped quietly.

Rules it's built on: 29 CFR part 5, 29 CFR part 3, 40 U.S.C. 3142, 40 U.S.C. 3702, DOL's WH-347 instructions and Michigan's Act 10 of 2023.

What it doesn't check

  • Whether each worker is in the right trade for the work they did.
  • Whether you have the right wage determination, or its latest changes.
  • Whether your fringe plans count, or their hourly credit. It uses the numbers you type.
  • Gross pay, deductions and net pay.
  • Apprentice pay steps, and whether each apprentice is registered.
  • Hours worked off this job in the same week. Those count toward overtime too.
  • Overtime for a worker split across two trades. It tells you when to check that by hand.
  • Michigan double-time pay, start and end times, and trades whose schedules set their own overtime rules.
  • Due dates, and other states' prevailing wage rules.

It checks math against the rates you type. It doesn't decide which trade a job belongs to, and it isn't a lawyer. If something doesn't fit your job, ask the contracting officer or the agency named in your contract.

For Michigan contractors

Michigan certified payroll went online on 2 April 2026

Checked 8 November 2026

On Michigan state projects, contractors and subcontractors now send certified payroll to the state's online system instead of the contracting agent. It's due no later than 10 days after each pay period ends. That rule is MCL 408.1122, part of the prevailing wage law Michigan brought back in 2023 Public Act 10 and amended in 2024 Public Act 110.

The 10-day clock

The 10 days start when the pay period ends. For a pay week that ends Saturday, 7 November 2026, day 10 is Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

Federal jobs have a different clock.

The WH-347 goes in within 7 days after the regular payday, under 29 CFR 3.4.

The starting point matters. Michigan counts from the end of the pay period. Federal jobs count from the regular payday.

The portal

Michigan's labor department, LEO, runs the system. The Online Certified Payroll Submission button on LEO's prevailing wage page opens an LCPtracker portal at userportal.fortifyv2.lcptracker.net. To get in, you register with LEO's Wage and Hour Division, which costs $500 a year, and you register each project. LEO then sends the steps to log in.

What the state's system asks for

  • Each worker's trade
  • Apprentice, journeyman or other level
  • Gross wages for the pay period
  • Hours worked each day
  • Start and end time each day
  • Hourly wage rate, hourly overtime rate and hourly fringe rate

It leaves out home addresses, phone numbers and Social Security numbers. When you submit, you sign electronically that the record is complete and accurate, and that you paid at least the required wage and fringe rates. You also confirm you understand that breaking the rule can lead to a suspended or revoked state project registration. LEO can fine up to $5,000 for each violation, plus 10%, under MCL 408.1113.

Overtime on Michigan rate schedules

LEO's rates page says every trade follows these rules unless its schedule notes otherwise:

  • Time and a half for hours over 8 in a day, or over 10 where four 10-hour days are allowed.
  • Time and a half for hours over 40 in a week, and for Saturdays.
  • Double time for Sundays and recognized holidays.

LEO updates its rates every year on 1 November. Tick Michigan state project in the checker to count these hours.

Which jobs it covers

State-funded work on public buildings, schools, roads, bridges and other public works, plus wind, solar and storage projects of 2 megawatts or more, private ones included. Contracts that already carry federal Davis-Bacon rates are exempt from the Michigan law. So are projects that send certified payroll to MDOT, and routine maintenance on energy projects.

Federal jobs use the WH-347

The U.S. Department of Labor gives the WH-347 away free, as a PDF and as an online form that runs in your browser. The online form does the pay math, one worker at a time. DOL's form pages don't mention checking your pay against your wage determination's rates. That's the part this page does. Get the form from DOL's WH-347 page, or use the online WH-347.

Sources, read 8 November 2026: MCL 408.1101, 408.1102, 408.1113 and 408.1122 in Michigan's Act 10 of 2023; LEO's prevailing wage and rates pages on michigan.gov; 29 CFR 3.4; and DOL's WH-347 pages.

Questions before you start

A few things worth asking.

Is it free?

Yes. Count Twice is free to use. There is nothing to install and no account.

Does my payroll file get uploaded?

No. Your browser reads your payroll file on this computer. It isn't uploaded or saved. Close the tab and it's gone. The page counts visits and button presses, never what's in your file.

What file does it need?

One week of payroll as a CSV file. Use the columns listed on this page, or start from the sample file. Type each trade's base rate and fringe from the wage determination in your contract.

Does it fill out the WH-347 for me?

No. It checks the numbers you put on the form. Use DOL's free WH-347 PDF or online form to fill it out. You still sign the Statement of Compliance yourself.

Does it work for Michigan state projects?

Yes. Tick Michigan state project to count Michigan's standard overtime hours. It doesn't check double-time pay, start and end times, trade-specific overtime rules or due dates. It doesn't make a portal upload file.

Does it check apprentice pay?

It checks the daily apprentice ratio you type from the registered program. It doesn't check apprentice pay steps, overtime pay or whether an apprentice is registered. An apprentice over the ratio gets the full rate for the work.

Can it tell me which trade a worker belongs in?

No. It checks math against the rates you type. It can't decide which trade fits the work or which wage determination applies. Ask the contracting officer or the agency named in your contract.
